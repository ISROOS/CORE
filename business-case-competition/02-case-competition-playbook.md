# Business Case Competition Playbook: how competitions are run, judged and won

Reference file built from official rulebooks (JMSB MBA ICC, HKICC, Hult Prize, L'Oréal Brandstorm, UGM-PKT BCC, KBMK, ITS MANIFEST, HSBC Indonesia BCC), published rubrics, coaching guides, and the GitHub repos listed in Sources. Every number is tied to the competition it comes from. Treat it as a pattern library. **The current year's rulebook for your own competition always wins.**

---

## 1. Typical formats (what organisers actually specify)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **John Molson MBA ICC** (Montreal, 2027 rules) | Regular case: 3 h. Live case: 2 h 30 after a ≤40-min company briefing and 45-min Q&A. Short case: 60 min | PowerPoint | Regular/live: 25 min + 15 min Q&A. Short case: 8-min briefing + 10-min Q&A, **one slide only**[1] | Teams of 4 (+1 alternate). **Patterns across these:**
- **Two big format families.** (a) *Lock-in sprints*: 3–5 h or 24 h, no internet at JMSB, 25-min or 10-min decks. (b) *Indonesian-style multi-stage*: written paper/proposal (10–15 pages) or exec summary → semifinal deck/video → live final of ~10 min + 10–15 min Q&A.
- **Slide caps are real and punitive.** HKICC gives a score of 1 for exceeding the cap[3]. JMSB's short case allows one slide[1].
- **Live twists exist.** HKICC drops a twist question in Q&A[3]. JMSB live cases reveal the company only on the morning of the case[1]. HKICC finals let judges interrupt the presentation[3].

## 2. Judging rubrics (weights are competition-specific)

### Published weights

**John Molson MBA ICC.** Five categories, each scored 1–10, then multiplied[1]:

| Category | × | Share of 100 | What judges are told to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analysis & development of alternatives | 2 | 20% | Key issues identified. Credible and creative alternatives. Case data used. Assumptions stated and realistic |
| Recommendations | 2.5 | 25% | Each alternative evaluated against clear criteria. Realistic, pragmatic, consistent with the data |
| **Implementation** | **3** | **30%** | Realistic timeline, major milestones, mitigations for top risks, cost estimates / financial projections |
| Presentation | 1 | 10% | Organised, on time, professional. Consistent in the role assumed |
| Q&A period | 1.5 | 15% | Defends and builds on the recommendation. Concise answers tied to case data. Teammates support each other without overstepping |

The first tiebreaker is the Implementation score, then Implementation + Recommendations[1]. CoachNed's worked example: a team that is 3 raw points better on Analysis but weak on Implementation loses by 11 weighted points. Every raw point moved from Analysis to Implementation is worth +1 weighted point[2].

**UGM-PKT BCC 2022 (Indonesia).**[8]
- **Preliminary (paper + video):** case understanding / problem identification / assumptions 15. Data processing and analysis method (incl. stakeholder analysis) 35. Recommendation (analysis, solution, feasibility) 35. Conclusion 5. Writing quality 10.
- **BCC I and final, content:** 15 / 40 / 40 / 5.
- **Final, presentation scored separately:** 50% organisation, clarity and time management. 50% Q&A (defending arguments, creative answers, teamwork in answering, etiquette).

**KBMK 2020 final.** Content element 70% (60% in one category) plus presentation element 30%. The presentation element is split 50 for organisation/time and 50 for Q&A[9].

**ITS MANIFEST 2026 (high-school BCC).**[10]
- **Exec-summary round:** analysis & solution 60%, research & methodology 25%, language 10%, format 5%.
- **Proposal round:** analysis & comprehensive solution 65% (including impact estimates and operational-financial feasibility), research & methodology 25%, sentence structure 4%, format 5%.

**L'Oréal Brandstorm 2026.**[7]
- **Business criteria, 25 points:** Innovative, Sustainable, Inclusive, Feasible, Scalable, 5 points each.
- **Team criteria, 25 points:** Ambition, Resilience, Judgment, Empathy, Learning agility. Each is marked 0, 2 or 5 (strongly observed) from the video.

**Hult Prize.** Four universal criteria: **Team, Idea, Impact, Business Viability**. Impact is checked for alignment with at least one UN SDG. Criteria deepen in later rounds (execution, traction, scalability). Judges score individually, then deliberate and aim for consensus, with a majority vote as fallback. The scorecard is published to competitors[4][5].

**Other published rubrics.**
- **HSMAI Foundation case competition:** five criteria × 20 pts. Situation understanding, critical analysis of environment, theory/evidence behind the strategies, purpose & expression, presentation skills & visuals[19].
- **IEO 2024:** 1–10 bands (Exemplary 9–10 … Beginning 1–2) on problem understanding, information synthesis, and market/stakeholder analysis[20].
- **Micron BACC 2022:** 3 bands (1–3 / 4–6 / 7–10) per question.
  - Q1 rewards "all claims are justified", "all assumptions clearly presented" and "all claims cited".
  - Q2 penalises "off the shelf" ideas versus ideas "designed to address the problem".
  - Q3 is a pure correctness check (LP formulation; 5 + 5 marks for the right numbers)[21].

**Non-official generic estimate (label it as such).** Hacking the Case Interview gives "typical" weights: analysis 25–30%, business acumen 20–25%, creativity 15–20%, presentation 20–25%, Q&A 10–15%[14]. Use these only when no rubric is published.

**Data-science variant (Indonesia).** In DS competitions the prediction score is usually ~60%. The remaining ~40% is the report (prelims) and presentation (finals)[23].

### What the rubrics agree on
1. **Recommendation + feasibility/implementation is the biggest bucket**: JMSB 55%, UGM 35–40% on the solution, MANIFEST 60–65%.
2. **Q&A is explicitly scored**: JMSB 15%, UGM final 50% of the presentation score, KBMK 50% of the presentation element.
3. **Stated, realistic assumptions** appear in JMSB, UGM and BACC.
4. **Format compliance costs points**: MANIFEST 5%, HKICC slide cap = score of 1, UGM penalties.

## 3. Winning deck structure

The consensus spine (answer-first, Pyramid Principle)[13][14]:

1. **Executive summary.** Recommendation + 2–3 reasons + expected impact. "A judge who reads only this slide should understand your full answer."[14] It should argue, not describe. Name the action, the strongest evidence and the main risk or condition[13].
2. **Situation / problem definition** (≤2 slides). Use SCQ: situation, complication, question[14][13].
3. **Root cause / analysis** (≈4 slides). One takeaway per slide, stated in an **action title**. Reading only the titles should tell the whole argument[14][13].
4. **Alternatives considered → recommendation.** Score the options against explicit criteria. JMSB literally grades this[1]. Raise an option the case didn't hand you: 36% of 2,141 casebook answer keys reward "surfacing the unprompted alternative"[22].
5. **Implementation roadmap.** Phases, 30/60/90-day steps, owners, resources, milestones[14]. Under JMSB weights this deserves more prep time than analysis[2].
6. **Financials.** Investment, revenue/benefit, ROI/payback, with transparent assumptions and a sensitivity check[13]. "No financial analysis" is called a major red flag[14]. CoachNed: implementation needs "a number the panel can check"[2].
7. **Risks & mitigation.** Top 2–3 risks with mitigations[14]. State the one assumption that would flip the answer, and how you'd validate it (rewarded in 19% of answer keys)[22].
8. **KPIs / success metrics.** Each recommendation should name an owner, action and measure of progress[13].
9. **Appendix.** Financial model, market sizing, competitor research, analogous case studies. Rule of thumb: "anything that could answer a potential Q&A question"[16]. Judges notice padded appendices[18].

**Slide craft.**
- Clean visuals, one idea per slide. If a slide takes more than ~5 seconds to grasp, it's too cluttered[14].
- Label axes, units, periods and data sources[13].
- Prefer points, tables and graphs over paragraphs, especially when the first round is a pure online submission[18].

**Analytics-case variant (BACC 2022 champion, pre-university category).** The 37-page deck follows the question order: Q1 role/impact → Q2.1 forecasting → Q2.2 machine requirement → Q2.3 optimisation[21]. It shows:
- explicit "Simplifying Assumption" slides;
- both a mathematical and a deep-learning approach, including a candid "LSTM did not work when put to test" slide;
- an MLP "Limitations" slide (black box);
- a naive vs advanced solution.

This is evidence of the reasoning-and-assumptions criteria in its rubric[21].

## 4. Time plans

**3-hour case (JMSB-style), from CoachNed[2]:**

| Min | Block |
|---|---|
| 0–20 | Read alone. Agree in one sentence on the decision the client must make |
| 20–35 | Issue tree, 2–3 alternatives, one owner each |
| 35–85 | Analysis + numbers. Two exhibits with a figure judges can check |
| 85–100 | Score alternatives against stated criteria |
| 100–140 | Timeline, milestones, cost, payback, top-3 risks + mitigations |
| 140–165 | Build the deck answer-first. Recommendation on slide 2, appendix for questions |
| 165–180 | Rehearse open/close. Write 5 hostile questions and assign who answers each |

**24-hour and 48-hour sprints, from CaseTutor[13]:**

| Milestone | 24 h | 48 h |
|---|---|---|
| Align on decision & roles | h1 | h1–2 |
| Research, test key assumptions | h2–7 | h3–14 |
| Choose recommendation, storyline | h8–10 | h15–22 |
| Draft, check, finalise slides | h11–19 | h23–38 |
| Rehearse, revise, submit | h20–24 | h39–48 |

**Percentage split for a 24-h/weekend case, from HTCI[14]:** read & align 10–15%, research 20–25%, analysis & recommendation 25–30%, deck 20–25%, rehearsal & Q&A prep 15–20%.

**Time rules of thumb.**
- Spend the first 15–20 minutes reading and agreeing on the core question before touching slides[14].
- Give each research question an owner, a time cap and a stop point. Stop digging once new information is unlikely to change the decision[13].
- Submit early. HKICC advises ≥20 minutes before the deadline and only counts the last upload[3]. One Deloitte team submitted at 5:59 pm for a 6 pm deadline[17].

## 5. Team roles

- **HTCI's four roles**[14]:
  - *Strategist*: framing, and checking the answer hits the prompt.
  - *Analyst*: numbers, models, ROI.
  - *Communicator*: narrative, transitions, leads Q&A.
  - *Designer*: deck.
  
  Roles can overlap, but each one needs a clear owner.
- CaseTutor: one coordinator owns decisions and the storyline. Teammates own research, analysis and slides, with set handoff times[13].
- **Everyone presents.** This is required at JMSB and HKICC[1][3]. One person presenting while three stand silent "signals poor teamwork"[14].
- **Diversity helps.** Cornell's Deloitte-winning team deliberately mixed a civil engineer, an actuary, a financier and a consultant, which paid off on a smart-city case[17].
- **Team size varies.** 4 (JMSB, HKICC), 3 (Brandstorm, UGM max), 2–3 (INFEST), 2 (KBMK, MANIFEST)[1][3][7][8][12][9][10].

## 6. Common mistakes

From judges and coaches[13][14][2]:
- **Solving a different problem / scope creep.** Read the prompt three times. Depth beats breadth[14].
- **Framework dumping** (SWOT + 4P + Porter with no decision link)[13].
- **Research with no decision question**[13].
- **A late, uncosted implementation plan.** CoachNed sees two hours on the issue tree and "a timeline pasted in at minute 170 with no cost on it"[2].
- **No financial analysis**, untraceable numbers, contradictory figures across slides[14][13].
- **Descriptive slide titles**, and cluttered text-heavy slides[13][14].
- **Overcomplicated multi-phase solutions** that are easy to attack in Q&A[14].
- **Skipping rehearsal, ignoring Q&A prep, going over time**[14]. HKICC and UGM judges stop you at time[3][8].
- **Breaking format rules**: slide caps, page limits, fonts, file naming[3][10][8].
- **Unequal participation** among team members[14].

## 7. Q&A defence tactics

**What judges are probing.** Whether the recommendation is well supported, financially understood, operationally realistic, resilient under uncertainty, owned by the whole team, and adaptable[15].

**Question types to prepare for**[15]:
- clarification;
- validation;
- stress test / "what if";
- alternative perspective;
- trade-off;
- assumption;
- curiosity, which is often an invitation rather than an attack.

**Process: LPTAC = Listen → Pause → Think → Answer → Confirm.** A brief pause reads as thoughtfulness[15].

**Spoken structure: Answer → Evidence → Implication**[15].
- Start with yes, no, a number or a position.
- If the honest answer is "it depends", say what it depends on and give the threshold.
- For "what if" questions, use **Change → Effect → Response** instead of rejecting the scenario.
- For "why not X?", compare against your decision criteria.
- Never invent a precise number.

**When you don't know**, say what you know, what you can reasonably infer, what you would learn, and how the decision is protected. Don't make "more research" the answer to everything[15].

**Team mechanics.**
- One primary responder per question; add-ons only when they add something.
- No contradictions between teammates.
- Don't try to "defeat" the judge[15].
- Signpost answers ("There are two reasons…")[16].
- JMSB explicitly scores collaboration "without overstepping"[1].

**Preparation.**
- Write 20+ tough questions on assumptions, financials and risks[14].
- Prepare "the ten questions you hope they don't ask", red-team the recommendation, and practise answering without slides[15].
- For every major number, know its source, the assumption it rests on, and what happens if it moves[13].
- Have backup slides ready. Pulling up the right appendix slide on demand impresses judges[14][16].
- Clarifying or restating a question buys thinking time. Be transparent about limitations[26].

## 8. Indonesia-specific notes
- **Typical flow.** Administrative selection → preliminary written output (exec summary / 10–15-page paper or proposal, often + video) → semifinal → live final of ~10 min + 10–15 min Q&A[8][9][10][12].
- **Rubrics are often public in the guidebook**, with explicit weights (see §2). Map every slide or section to a scored line.
- **Writing quality and format are scored.** UGM 10%, MANIFEST 5–15%. Formal Indonesian (EYD) applies to Indonesian-language entries[8][10].
- **Corporate-run BCCs** such as HSBC Indonesia use multi-panel knockouts with 3 judges per panel, and may give a **new case** in later rounds. Practise fresh-case speed, not just polishing one case[11].
- **Related Chinese competitions** (e.g. IMA management-accounting case, MPAcc case contests) list the defence themes as data measurement, feasibility and Q&A[24].

## 9. One-page checklist
- [ ] Read the rubric and rules first. Build a slide budget proportional to the weights[13][2].
- [ ] State the decision question in one sentence (SCQ)[14].
- [ ] Generate 2–3 alternatives, including one the case didn't hand you. Score them against stated criteria[1][22].
- [ ] Recommendation on slide 1–2. Action titles throughout[14][2].
- [ ] Costed roadmap + KPIs + top-3 risks/mitigations + breaking assumption[1][22].
- [ ] Checkable numbers with sources, units and a sensitivity check[13].
- [ ] Appendix mapped to anticipated questions[16].
- [ ] Rehearse ≥3 full timed runs[14]. Mock Q&A using LPTAC / A-E-I[15].
- [ ] Comply with format rules. Submit ≥20 minutes early[3].
