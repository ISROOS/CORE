## 2. Analysis of the best entries

### A. FOCUS Foods, HBS NVC 2015 SE Winner (deck + exec summary)
- **Format:** 20 slides in 4:3. The 5-page exec summary works as a mini-plan (the full plan was only available by email).
- **Storyline:** environmental cost of farming → aquaponics as the solution → the system and the rooftop render → business model as a 4-step staircase → value chain → cost advantage → "everyone benefits" → production capacity → product mix by year → roadmap → financials → environmental and public-health impact → team → vision → closing logo.
- **Signature techniques:**
  - **Removing steps from the value chain**: of the 8 conventional farm steps, 5 are struck out, leaving seed → harvest → already at the retailer.
  - A **cost-advantage table**: each source of advantage gets a % improvement and a 5-year cumulative $ figure, summed to about $23M. This supports a 25% operating margin versus 10% for conventional farms.
  - An **anchor customer that is also the site**: the greenhouse sits on the roof of a partner store in a food desert. That gives guaranteed offtake and zero transport, and the partner's 296 stores become the expansion story.
  - A **roadmap with "we are here" and "Harvard would help get us here" arrows**, so the prize is tied to a specific milestone.
  - **Impact converted into equivalents** (tonnes of CO₂ shown as everyday equivalents).
- **Design:** white background, a teal/green fish-and-leaf logo, the brand acronym spelled out as a tagline (Fresh. Organic. Community-oriented. Urban. Sustainable.), simple clip-art, and green bar charts. It looks plain by today's standards. **It won on the model and the partner, not on design.**
- **Why it won:** a concrete anchor partner, a clear structural cost advantage that is quantified, double impact (environment and food deserts), and a staged capital ask ($400k → $2.4M).

### B. Barakat Bundle, HBS NVC 2015 SE Runner-up (deck + 27-page plan)
- **Storyline:** title with slogan → full-bleed photo with a mother's quote → scale of the problem in big numbers → "effective solutions already exist" (cause × intervention matrix) → proven foreign model adapted locally → product → conditional-receipt mechanism (the kit is given only after prenatal check-ups) → funding-to-delivery chain → existing partners → barriers addressed (affordability, accessibility, education) → outcomes → team and advisers → **pilot-to-scale map** (pilot → entry → growth with bundle counts and % share) → **price as a % of per-capita income** versus competitors → profitable from Year 1 → "beyond profits: about $250M social value" (deaths averted, productivity) → closing quote → appendix (P&L, cash flow, operating assumptions, competitor and risk table).
- **Techniques worth stealing:** a **proven-model transplant** (Finland → South Asia) to reduce perceived risk; an **incentive-design mechanism**; a **social-ROI slide** that turns impact into $; an **operating-assumptions appendix** with a year-by-year subsidy mix and staff count; and a **risk table** covering security, terrorism and corruption.
- **Design:** watercolour splash background, photography of real mothers, a serif/sans mix, and icon rows. Emotional open, numeric middle, emotional close.

### C. Tomato Jos, HBS NVC 2014 SE Runner-up (deck + 29-page plan); now a real company
- **Storyline:** full-bleed field photo → **"Meet Azumi"** (a farmer persona with hectares and crops) → "Trapped in a cycle of poverty" (3 causes) → a **$2.5B market waterfall** (demand → grown → sold → imported → gap) → mission and solution → farm → transport → market flow → team with on-the-ground experience → competitive-advantage flywheel → **farmer income by tier** (baseline / bronze / silver / gold yield) → **unit economics** (variable cost per hectare) → **3 phases to profitability** (capacity t/day, % outgrowers, yield) → navigating the Nigerian context (operational, political, regulatory, conflict) → bottom-line growth → triple bottom line KPIs (Year 5 vs baseline) → funding growth (Year 1 $100k → Year 2 $500k → Year 4 $3M with sources of finance) → competitive landscape → appendix pro-formas.
- **Techniques:** a persona hook, a market waterfall that *shows* the gap, tiered farmer outcomes, a phase/capacity/outgrower table, a **country-risk slide**, and **sources of finance by stage** (grants → impact debt → equity).
- **Design:** red tomato photography as the backdrop, white cards, a red/green palette taken from the logo, and clean consultant-style charts.

### D. 2MBA Inc. and Fabrica, MOOT CORP World Champions (full written plans, c.1999–2000)
- **Format:** classic VC plan, roughly 25–30 printed pages + appendices A–I.
- **2MBA structure:** exec summary → **"The Offer" as chapter 1**: a $1.5M ask in **two tranches tied to a milestone** (the first released when the Nestlé contract signs). It sets out equity split, IRR about 93% and an 18× cash return in Year 5, inventor equity + royalty, and minimum-volume clauses. Then products → organisation → strategic analysis (macro/industry/competitors) → marketing → production → organisation charts for Year 1 vs Year 5 → financial plan with an explicit assumptions list → appendices: **Bell-Mason dimension analysis** (venture-maturity scoring), **critical risks rated Zero/Low/Moderate**, a SWOT turned into a strategy matrix ("capitalise on strengths…"), testimonials, production layout, action plan and team details.
- **Fabrica:** a story opening (why sampling is broken for weaving mills) → founder origin → product specs → industry (they argue they *create* a market, so the whole weaving industry is the frame) → sales targets by country → "why we sell samples, not looms" (an explicit choice NOT to do something) → full statements: income, cash flow, balance sheet, shareholder table, plus a **monthly P&L for Year 1**. Gross margins of 56–81% are justified line by line.
- **Why they won:** the investor's deal terms come first, the downside is managed by milestone tranches, a blue-chip channel partner removes demand risk, and the financials are fully integrated.

### E. HERO, Hult Prize 2026 Top 20 (3 slides + judge commentary)
- **Format:** a 2-minute pitch with very few words per slide.
- **Slide 1:** a dark full-bleed hospital photo, with one sentence in bold white Poppins-style type and the second half in **red italic** ("…life stops").
- **Sequence:** problem → solution (what it is → why better → clinical proof) → traction **timeline** (grants → prototype → patent pending → 25+ physician interviews → **LOI worth $3M**) → team plus advisers → **closer that uses the pitch length as the argument** ("in these 2 minutes, X lives could have been saved").
- **Name:** HERO is a backronym that describes the product.
- **BAGYT (Top 80):** funnels the problem from a global number to one country to one unserved person. Every feature is written as a user benefit. Evidence goes on the same slide as the claim (canes already sold and tested). Wins on proof over promises.

### F. Redish, claimed Juara 1 (Unpad, 2022): the Indonesian proposal archetype
- **Format:** 31-page A4 proposal: cover → foreword → table of contents → infographic exec summary → Chapter I (background, idea) → Chapter II (vision/mission, goals, benefits, product, **app prototype screens**, SWOT, competitor analysis, HR plan with job descriptions, market analysis: STP + 4P + **AIDA funnel**) → Chapter III (operations, app user flow, financial plan) → references → appendix.
- **Design:** a sage-green border frame on every page, a cream/white body, a logo watermark and section badge, and yellow/orange financial tables. The exec summary is set as a 2-column magazine layout with a photo.
- **Assessment:** typical of campus BPC winners. Standard frameworks, an app mockup, and readable finance tables. It is weaker than Wijaya on root-cause logic, sizing and sensitivity analysis. Useful as a baseline for what campus judges reward, not as a ceiling.

### G. Others in brief
- **FlyAI** (2nd, 6 slides): navy background, a "why / if you need… then us" slide, 3 value-proposition icons, a demo screenshot, team faces. Hackathon minimum.
- **10KSB finalist** (42 slides): a 3-step framework (Consistency → Campaigning → Compressing) with step divider slides, screenshots with red circles as evidence, analytics (95.8% bounce rate) as the problem proof, and a "How much will all this cost me?" slide answered with free tools. ## 3. Cross-entry patterns and what is NEW versus the Wijaya patterns file

**Shared by every verified winner:**
1. The opening is a hook: a persona ("Meet Azumi"), a full-bleed photo with a quote, or a one-line emotive problem. Big numbers follow the hook and never replace it.
2. The problem is **funnelled**: global stat → country → one person/place (BAGYT, Tomato Jos waterfall).
3. **Anchor partner / channel = demand de-risking** (ShopRite roof, Nestlé contract, Midwifery Association + J&J, physician LOIs).
4. **Staged money**: tranches tied to milestones (2MBA), stage-by-stage sources of finance (Tomato Jos), "we are here → prize gets us here" (FOCUS). Wijaya already does stage gates and should keep doing so.
5. Full-sentence or claim titles ("Losing out on a $2.5 billion market", "Profitable and sustainable from Year 1").
6. **Heavy appendix** with pro-formas and an assumptions table. The same pattern as Wijaya's thin-main/heavy-appendix approach, which confirms it.

**NEW (not in `05-wijaya-team-patterns.md`):**
- **Rubrics weight the TEAM heavily**: HBS NVC gives Team **30%**, the largest single weight. Rice and Oregon ask "is this a team I'd invest in?" and "do they know their gaps?". Wijaya entries treat team as a closing slide. Add a "why us" slide covering domain proof, roles mapped to the gaps, named advisers, and commitment (full-time, money invested).
- **Traction and validation is a scored category** (HBS 15%; Hult judges): LOIs, pilots, pre-orders, a prototype on a **timeline**. Replace survey-only validation with at least 1–2 signed LOIs or MoUs and a traction timeline.
- **Q&A is a big share of the score** (VE: 20 of 100 points in the presentation, and the presentation is 70% of the total; Oregon gives 30 minutes with Q&A; Rice runs heavy due diligence). Build a judge Q&A bank and backup slides.
- **"The Offer" up front**: put the investor's terms in chapter 1 (amount, equity, use, tranches, IRR/multiple, exit). Wijaya ends with the ask; the MOOT champions lead with it.
- **Social-ROI slide**: impact turned into $ and lives (Barakat's $250M, FOCUS's impact equivalents), with triple-bottom-line KPIs showing Year 5 vs baseline.
- **Proven-model transplant** ("worked in Finland, adapted for X") to lower perceived risk.
- **Mechanism design**: the benefit is conditional on a desired behaviour (a kit for check-ups), and outgrower tiers raise yields.
- **Country/regulatory risk slide** (Tomato Jos, Barakat): political, security, corruption and infrastructure risks, each with a mitigation. Wijaya's risk work is operational (FMEA); add a context or regulatory layer.
- **Value-chain step removal** and **cost-advantage decomposition** (each advantage → % → $ over 5 years) as moat proof.
- **Closer that turns the pitch format into the argument** (HERO's "in these 2 minutes…"). Compare Wijaya's persona bookend.
- **Rice does not score the written plan.** At pitch-format competitions, invest in the deck and Q&A. Indonesian PKM/KBMI and campus BPCs **do** score the paper, with heavy weight on data accuracy and depth of argument (SAF 50%).
- **Indonesian funding schemes (PKM-K/KBMI) weight "potensi profit + keberlanjutan" at 35% and product advantage at 20%**. Budget and schedule are only 10%, so a fancy RAB earns little.

**Where Wijaya is already ahead of these winners:** sensitivity/scenario grids (none of the winners have them), formula-tagged numbers, the assumption register, and design polish. The 2014–15 HBS decks are plain by 2026 standards. Gaps to close: team proof, external traction (LOIs), deal terms, and social ROI.

## 4. Search sources for next time
- **HBS NVC "Tools" pages** (`/social-enterprise-track/tools`, `/business-track/judging`): past winners' exec summaries, decks and plans, plus the rubric. This was the best single source.
- **MOOT CORP archive**: `web.archive.org/web/2005id_/http://www.businessplans.org/businessplans.html`, which lists about 30 plans with "Best of the Best" topic picks. Fetch slowly: Wayback throttles parallel requests, so use sequential requests with retries across several timestamps (`mootcorp/crawl2.py`).
- Texas ScholarWorks "Venture Labs Investment Competition (formerly Moot Corp)" collection: a large archive of plans, but CloudFront blocks it (403 for both urllib and the browser). Retry later or from another network.
- Rice BPC `/competitor-resources` (scoring criteria, rules) and results pages per year.
- goruworld.com blog: breakdowns of Hult Prize decks with slides, written by a Hult judge.
- GitHub organiser repos (`tum-ai/makeathon_pitchdecks`), `midovislam/awesome-pitch-decks` (754 decks on Drive), and `GyaneshSamanta/Case-Competitions` (case comps, including GCCH winners).
- Speakerdeck: offers direct `files.speakerdeck.com/...pdf` downloads. Search for "finalist" or "winner".
- SlideShare/Scribd: many Hult and campus decks, but most are not winners and downloads need login. Use the browser to read the Scribd text layer (`.text_layer`).
- Indonesia: university news pages (ugm.ac.id, sbm.itb.ac.id, prasetiyamulya.ac.id/ceritaprasmul, tazkia.ac.id, unair, its.ac.id/mb achievements, award.binus.ac.id) for verified juara and the idea description. Rulebooks come from kabarlomba, infolomba and linktr.ee booklets. Use PKM/KBMI panduan from simbelmawa.kemdiktisaintek.go.id and campus repositories (repository.*.ac.id) for KBMI/PKM proposals.
- Search queries that worked: `"winning business plan" site:edu`, `scribd "juara 1" "business plan"`, `site:speakerdeck.com competition winner`, `<competition> judging criteria pdf`.
