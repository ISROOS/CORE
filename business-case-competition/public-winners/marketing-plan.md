# Public winning MARKETING PLAN / brand / marketing case entries: benchmark library

Compiled Nov 2026. Files are in `public-winners/marketing-plan/`, and `manifest.json` there lists 20 downloaded or saved items. All of these are third-party works kept for private study. The notes below are paraphrased summaries; no slide text has been copied.

**Bottom line**
- **Best free source of verified marketing-plan winners:** DECA. It publishes *ICDC winning written projects* for Integrated Marketing Campaign Product, Service and Event: 7 PDFs from 2025, plus three 2026 samples in the new 20-slide format.
- **International case-competition winners:** only available through SlideShare uploads, where the uploader makes the claim, or occasionally on an official site (CBS Case Competition hosts its 2024 winner deck).
- **Indonesian marketing-plan and marketing-case winners almost never publish their decks.** Searches of university news, Scribd, SlideShare, Behance, GitHub and Issuu found none. What *is* public is university news write-ups that describe the winning concept, acronym and process. Those are catalogued in table B as view-only.

## 2. Analysis of the best entries

### 2.1 DECA ICDC winning IMC written plans (2025): Honey and Mackie's, Happify, Bee's Wrap, Ring
- **Format:** strict 10 pages plus title, table of contents, bibliography and appendix. Every winner follows the rubric headings in the same order, numbered with Roman numerals: I Executive Summary · II Description · III Objectives · IV Target Market · V Activities & Schedule · VI Budget · VII Key Metrics · VIII Bibliography · IX Appendix. The judges score against these headings, so **mirroring the rubric is non-negotiable.** For 2025-26 DECA switched the format to a **20-slide pitch deck** with the same sections. The 2026 samples show a cover, an "about us" slide, and a table of contents with slide numbers per section.
- **Storyline:** problem stat → product/brand → 2–3 SMART objectives → primary / secondary / tertiary target personas (demographic, geographic, psychographic and behavioural columns, with a stock-photo face) → a campaign made of **named sub-campaigns** (#HappifyHelps, "Scoop-n-Score", "Flavor Competition", podcast series) → itemised budget table → KPI formulas.
- **Numbers:** budgets are small and itemised, priced per unit (CPM, CPC, booth fee, printing). They close with a **three-figure "result strip"**: total cost / projected revenue / ROI (Honey and Mackie's: about $15.7K cost, $42.8K revenue, 172% ROI; Ring reports ROI around 327%). Bee's Wrap writes each metric's **formula** (CTR, NPS, ROI) and gives current vs projected follower counts.
- **Design signature:** these are designed brochures, not Word documents. Each takes its palette from the client brand (Happify magenta/orange; Honey & Mackie's chocolate-drip header with pastel cards). Section titles are big rounded display type, content sits in cards, and there are ad mockups (Instagram post mock, booth render) and a **Gantt-style campaign calendar** in the appendix. The executive summary is a one-page infographic: description, goals, target-market chips, budget/revenue/ROI bubbles and a mini timeline.
- **Why they won:** complete rubric coverage, every objective tied to a metric, a credible itemised budget with ROI, and professional visual polish at high-school level.

### 2.2 CBS Case Competition 2024 Winner – Boozt.com (JAMS Consulting) ✅ official
- **Format:** 16:9, about 33 main slides plus a large appendix (73 slides in total). A section tracker in the footer (Executive Summary · Insights · Strategy · Impact) highlights the current section, and full-sentence action titles run throughout.
- **Storyline:** a one-page **SCQ-style executive summary** (Key Question / Situation / Solution / Impact rows with icons, where the impact is the headline number) → insights backed by customer data → a **persona's decision journey**: "Alma", a 35-year-old loyal mother, walks the CDJ path awareness → consideration → purchase → evaluation → loyalty, with a red ✕ pain point at each stage → an **impact × feasibility bubble matrix** of about 10 candidate tactics, with the chosen 3 highlighted → 3 pillars (brand perception pop-up/online campaign; AI discovery via a Google API; personalised retention) → phone mockups per pillar → roadmap → impact.
- **Numbers:** a consolidated NPV table (revenue lines per pillar, cost lines, discounting, WACC 5.7%), a cash-flow chart, giant callouts (**3,474 SEKm NPV, 7.7% CAGR**), a **"key inputs" slide** listing the assumption behind each pillar (for example conversion %, number of API calls, lift %), and a **WACC × growth-CAGR sensitivity heat-map** with the base case boxed.
- **Design:** white background, teal/navy accent, client wordmark top right, photo-overlay section dividers, presenter names on the dividers.
- **Why it won:** a hard key question with a target ("grow revenue 40% by 2027"), a promise to beat it ("104%"), and a visible, assumption-traceable path from tactic to NPV.

### 2.3 Univ. of Melbourne × elevio, 1st place (MINT 2017) – B2B SaaS marketing plan
- **Storyline:** team intro → agenda (situation / interpretation of task / proposal / measurement) → situation statistics → a **"what you said" slide that restates the client brief as three verbs** (Identify / Reach / Engage) → target-audience **pyramid** (B2B decision-makers → influencers → industries → SMEs/large enterprises → geography) → two analogue **case studies** (Optus, DHL) → objectives → plan overview → **key message per segment** → a **three-period timeline** (Growth → Transition → Retention) → channel ranking chart → a tactics matrix by channel family (Web content, Social, Research, Direct) → one page per tactic, each with a 3-column period table → budget table (about $40K, unit-costed) → thank you.
- **Design device:** every tactic slide ends with a **blue "evidence strip" footer** of 2–4 supporting statistics. Large uppercase section-divider slides. Restrained white/charcoal with brand blue/purple tiles.
- **Why it won:** client-centric framing (it literally plays the brief back), segment-specific messaging, and a phased plan with costed channels.

### 2.4 L'Oréal Brandstorm – Team Canada 2014 (Kiehl's men's body grooming) and Brazil 2009 (Maybelline)
- **Canada:** the cover *is* the hook, a James-Bond-style chest-hair visual later reprised as a **bookend on the last slide**. Then cultural shift (the male body ideal, athletes grooming) → Google Trends "manscaping" curve, Amazon bestseller proof, market-by-geography table → **"Blue Ocean opportunity" slide** → a 3-SKU product line with ingredient icons → men's product geography map → strategic fit for Kiehl's (4 chevron reasons) → pricing ladder (€14–34 plus a starter kit) → 3 target markets → communication objectives → launch: window display, in-store activation, sampling funnel (deluxe → sachet → gratis), PR in men's magazines, athletic partnerships, country-website takeover mockups, social → a **360° launch wheel** recap.
- **Brazil 2009:** a consumer-insight quote leads into a product invention (an earring-clutch fragrance dispenser), then wear-tech and biodegradable-material detail, then a heavy **retail-execution gallery** (counter display, gondola, shop-in-shop and outdoor-projection renders, press kit, product placement), ending on "Coming soon".
- **Pattern:** Brandstorm finals reward a **tangible product plus a complete go-to-market world**: rendered packaging, merchandising renders and media mockups. Financial depth is light, since price points stand in for a P&L. Each deck is fully art-directed in the brand's own look (Kiehl's black/blue apothecary; Maybelline pink/black).

### 2.5 Unilever FLL 2013 Final – Team Russia (Lifebuoy "Help a child reach 5")
- One idea split into **5 numbered levers in two groups** (Involvement 1–3, Sharing 4–5): celebrity movie, NGO creation, future-leaders student movement, employee power, TED talk. A **progress bar of the 5 icons with the current lever lit** recurs as a tracker before each section. Mostly picture-led slides with one-line headers.
- Closes with a **reach multiplier**: employees × share rate × friends × view rate gives the potential audience (about 3.46 million). It is a simple, memorable "back of envelope" number.
- **Lesson:** in a live global final, a single clear tracker plus one closing big number beats dense analysis.

### 2.6 HubSpot 1st place (2012), short version
- Consulting-style deck in 19 slides: agenda → core competencies → goals → decisions (outbound? segmentation) → a named **two-segment persona pair** ("Marketer Marys" vs "Owner Ollies") → customer lifetime value table per segment → pricing segmentation → org structure → performance analysis (EBITDA, IRR) → hedonic pricing. Plain template. It won on a clever named tactic ("lukewarm calling") and segment-level unit economics.

## 3. Cross-entry patterns and what is NEW compared with the Wijaya patterns file

**Patterns shared with Wijaya's house style:** a branded acronym or named pillars (FIT at ANMPC, 5A+ at EURECA), a persona hook (Alma in CBS), mockups per pillar, and a one-page executive summary.

**New techniques to adopt:**
1. **Mirror the rubric headings one-to-one, numbered in the judge's order** (DECA). If an Indonesian MPC publishes a scoring sheet, use its exact section names as slide or page titles so judges can tick boxes.
2. **SCQ executive-summary grid** (Key Question / Situation / Solution / Impact, each as one icon row; CBS). It is tighter than Wijaya's 5–6 block infographic and makes the key question carry a numeric target.
3. **Persona decision-journey map with a pain point at each stage** (CBS "Alma"). Each red ✕ maps to a pillar. This upgrades Wijaya's persona device from story to diagnostic.
4. **Impact × feasibility bubble matrix of about 10 candidate tactics, with the chosen ones highlighted and the rest greyed** (CBS). It is the visual cousin of MetaMind's scoring table and works better on a slide.
5. **A "Key inputs" slide** that lists, per pillar, the 3–4 operating assumptions driving the financials (conversion %, lift %, number of stores), placed beside a **WACC × growth heat-map**. Wijaya already does sensitivity; the new part is the one-glance inputs-per-pillar panel that judges can challenge.
6. **"What you said" brief playback** (Melbourne). Restate the client's ask as 3 verbs and reuse those verbs as section names, so the client feels heard.
7. **Phased plan by customer lifecycle (Growth → Transition → Retention)** with *every* tactic page showing the same 3-column period table (Melbourne). It gives consistency across many channels.
8. **Evidence strip footer**: 2–4 supporting statistics with sources on every tactic slide (Melbourne). It is a lighter-weight alternative to Wijaya's appendix citations.
9. **Key message per segment**, with distinct copy for each persona (Melbourne, HubSpot "Marketer Marys / Owner Ollies"). Use alliterative persona names.
10. **Cost / Revenue / ROI "result strip"** as three bubbles at the end of the budget page *and* in the executive summary (DECA). Write the KPI formula next to each metric (CTR = clicks ÷ impressions) and show current vs projected baselines, which fixes the "KPIs without baselines" weakness noted in the Wijaya file.
11. **Named sub-campaigns with hashtags** (#HappifyHelps, Scoop-n-Score, Paw Bomb Drama, #SekotakSusuAjaDulu, Glow with Tropicana). Marketing-plan judges remember campaign names, not pillar letters. Give every pillar a consumer-facing campaign name in addition to the strategic acronym.
12. **Campaign calendar / Gantt in the appendix** with colour per activity type (DECA).
13. **Brandstorm-style "world building"**: for product-innovation marketing cases, render the product, the shelf or display, the website takeover and the press kit. A **360° launch wheel** closes the deck, and the opening visual is **bookended** on the last slide.
14. **Lever tracker**: a row of numbered icons with the current one lit, as a section tracker (UFLL). Use it as an alternative to the bottom tab bar when the solution is a list of levers.
15. **Reach multiplier math** (employees × share% × friends × view%) as a closing big number (UFLL). It is quick and transparent; add the formula line.
16. **Statistical tests on primary survey data** (t-test, ANOVA by Aksara, the FEB UI MIST 2025 winner) and a **functional Figma or UI demo plus role-play in the pitch** (BIZBUDS). Indonesian judges explicitly praised the "tech + data-driven" approach.
17. **Real-world validation**: experts (salon stylists, pharmacists for Brandstorm Maya), a foreign-market professional (UFLL ITB), and an actual implementation sprint (Frisian Flag). Interviews with domain experts are what lifted these entries from finalist to winner.
18. **Iteration pays**: Team Maya went from runner-up to national champion on a second attempt, and Aksara went from 4th seed to 1st by reworking for the final. This matches the Wijaya JELANTRA → JELANTARA lesson.

**Weaknesses seen in winners** (so beating them is possible): DECA ROI figures are often naive (projected revenue ÷ cost, with no COGS or incrementality; the Fashion for the Cure event entry quotes "ROI: 10,028" without stating a unit). Brandstorm decks have almost no P&L. Melbourne has no KPIs page. Wijaya's formula-backed, scenario-tested numbers remain a differentiator, so keep them but present them in the CBS style (inputs panel + heat-map + 2 giant callouts).
