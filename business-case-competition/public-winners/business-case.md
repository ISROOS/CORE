## 2. Per-entry analysis (best 8)

### ① CBS Invitational 2026: L'Oréal Nordic male beauty (🥇 Precision Consulting)
- **Format.** 105 PDF pages, but many are animation builds exported as separate pages. That leaves about 30 unique main slides plus a 60-page appendix that opens with two appendix tables of contents (Analysis and Finance).
- **Storyline.** The deck opens on the scope/key question, then the executive summary (question → 2 strategies → 3 impact KPIs). The funnel analysis runs market → consumer trends → **named segment ("Modern Maintainers")** → category (skincare wins on a scoring matrix) → brand prioritisation (CeraVe → La Roche-Posay → Kiehl's, phased). A **persona, "Hannes"**, is walked through a 5-step customer journey with a pain point at each step. Each tactic is then mapped to the step it fixes, and a closing slide shows "Hannes after". Pillar 2 is a market-growing **startup accelerator**. The deck finishes with roadmap, costs, revenue, risk matrix and a repeat of the exec summary.
- **Frameworks.** Weighted decision matrices for category and brand, customer-journey pain-point mapping, the funnel (awareness → conversion → retention), and a probability × severity risk matrix.
- **Numbers.** Headline impact is a 54% market growth (target was 50%), 56% capture of that growth and €99M incremental revenue over 5 years, against a €28.3M total cost. The appendix has **per-country × per-channel revenue builds** (DK/NO/SE/FI × drugstore/grocery/online/ads), **sensitivity per brand**, and a **Monte Carlo simulation**. There is also an EU cosmetics-regulation check.
- **Design.** White background with the L'Oréal black/gold feel. Grey photo divider slides. A top tracker (ANALYSIS · FINANCIALS · RECOMMENDATIONS) plus the team name sits on every slide. Titles are full action sentences, and **continuation titles start with "…"** so the story reads across slides. A black "Key Takeaway" bar closes each slide, and real brand logos are used as chips.
- **Why it won (inferred).** The answer exceeds the client's target (54% vs 50%) and states it in the client's own metric. Scope narrows hard on evidence: one segment, one category, one hero brand, then a replicate playbook. The persona makes the tactics concrete, and a large appendix backs every number.

### ② CBS Invitational 2025: Nordea UHNW wealth transfer (🥇 PARC Consulting)
- **Format.** 62 pages: about 37 main (with builds) plus 22 numbered appendix slides (#1–#21).
- **Storyline.** The exec summary splits the key question into 3 sub-questions, each answered by one pillar. The analysis covers strengths, then segment sizing (business owners are the largest segment), then a **persona journey ("Entrepreneur Erik")** with pain points, then an **impact × feasibility matrix of 10 candidate ideas**. The strategy is named **"PET"** (01 Plan: succession planning · 02 Elevate: next-gen heir academy · 03 Transform: "Infinity Portal" family digital platform). After that come the impact timeline, revenue, risks ("bulletproofing") and the exec summary again.
- **Numbers.** About €111M revenue uplift for the UHNWI segment by 2030. The appendix holds client build-outs, implied portfolio mix and fees, a cost build with wage bands, and **2-variable sensitivity tables for each initiative** (e.g. summit spend × number of events → NPV).
- **Design.** Navy and Nordea blue on white. A bottom tracker (Executive Summary · Insights · Strategy · Impact) highlights the active section. Full-width building-photo dividers. A **strategy-objective chip** ("Strategic Objective: …") sits top-left on every pillar slide. The 3 pillars are shown as big numerals 01/02/03 over a dark photo, and that slide repeats as a progress map before each pillar.
- **Why it won.** The pillars map 1:1 to the sub-questions, the idea-selection matrix shows options were weighed, the persona carries the human story, and sensitivity is done per initiative rather than just once.

### ③ CBS Invitational 2024: Boozt.com (🥇 JAMS Consulting)
- **Format.** 73 pages: 39 main plus 31 numbered appendix slides with their own TOC.
- **Storyline.** The exec summary uses the **Key Question / Situation / Solution / Impact** rows. Analysis runs through strengths, the customer-frequency segments (active / moderate / non-frequent) and **persona "Alma"** with her journey pain points, then an **impact × feasibility 2×2 of 10 tactics**. The strategy is the acronym **"BAR"** (Boost brand perception · AI-enabled discovery · Retention via personalisation). It is shown as three cards, and the active card is highlighted on each section slide. After that come the risk matrix, a capability fit, NPV (3.5bn SEK, 7.7% CAGR) and inputs.
- **Techniques.** A working **prototype of an AI image-search feature ("Lenz") built on the Gemini API**, with API cost tables and a phone mock-up flow (snap → tap → buy). There is a GDPR compliance slide and a "why not reviews" slide that dismisses an obvious alternative.
- **Design.** Teal/dark-green palette. Section dividers use a team member's name, so presenter hand-offs are visible. Phone mock-ups are used heavily.
- **Caveat worth learning from.** The consolidated NPV attributes very large revenue to the AI feature from year 1. Even a 1st-place deck can carry aggressive attribution. Judges rewarded the concept and its rigour of form, and the number has not been verified.

### ④ CBS Invitational 2023: Vestas PtX (🥇 Causeway Consulting, HKU)
- **Format.** 59 pages: 30 main, then a "Slides Navigator" (index of the main deck and appendix) and 27 appendix slides.
- **Storyline.** The exec summary has Vision → 3 key questions → the "PtX" strategy (Piloting in EU · Together via JV · Xcceleration) → 3 impact KPIs (12% of EU green-H₂ supported, 13% EBIT margin, 20% ROCE by 2030). The value chain is shown as a 5-step strip, and the steps Vestas should own are highlighted on the next slide (a progressive reveal). A **region × criteria Harvey-ball/traffic-light table** covers 7 regions × 6 criteria, followed by a business-model comparison (JV vs project consortium vs licensing). Implementation is gated (secure orders → product → JV partner → pilot), with 3 key concerns and financials showing "no external financing needed".
- **Appendix.** A full 3-statement model (sales, FCF, balance sheet, P&L), regional deep-dives per criterion, "Why not China?", JV precedents and competitor moves.
- **Design.** Navy with a cyan accent. A tracker sits at the bottom with the team name and page number. Section dividers show a big circled number over a dark photo, and each presenter's name appears on the section divider.
- **Why it won.** The acronym doubles as the question structure. It also shows financial completeness (3-statement), a criteria-based market choice, and a de-risked staged entry.

### ⑤ CBS GLOBAL 2025: European Energy BESS (🥇 TrueSouth Consulting)
- **Format.** Short and dense: 13 main slides plus 3 appendix slides. The title-page subtitle is the question.
- **Storyline.** The exec summary uses challenge / solution / impact with 3 KPIs (1,705 MW, 15% IRR, €400M NPV) and a **year-by-year capital allocation** (Y1 retrofit DK + LT, Y2 Australia + German build-to-sell, Y3 finish + recycle cash). The analysis is followed by a **market-selection framework**: rank country × offtake combinations by NPV/MW, then overlay 4 contextual indices (negative-price, regulatory, competition, own-capability). The deck then gives a strategy per market, the timeline, a probability × impact risk grid and an NPV curve.
- **Distinctive.** The appendix shows **code**, a weighted-sentiment scoring function used to build an index, plus the NPV calculations for all 6 given combinations per country. The analysis is transparent and reproducible.
- **Design.** Teal on white with numbered slide labels (01, 02…) next to the title, and a top tracker of 5 sections. Very high information density, with 3–4 panels per slide.

### ⑥ CBS GLOBAL 2024: Berlingske PULSE (🥇 EDGE Consulting)
- **Format.** 20 slides with no separate appendix.
- **Storyline.** It **opens with the question as a full slide**, then the exec summary (Considerations / Question / Strategy / Impact rows; impact = DKK 5.79M by 2027 vs the 5M target, 2,100 new U30 subscribers/month). Market insights are given as big-number stat cards. An options matrix scores 4 options (forum, VR, reels, personal journalist) on 5 criteria with Harvey balls. The solution "PULSE" has 3 sub-brands: PULSE in 5, PULSE Points and PULSE Beyond. There is a technical architecture slide (LLM pipeline) and **proof-by-analogy case studies** (other publishers' results with AI personalisation). Go-to-market tactics follow, then financials, risks, a monthly Gantt and the exec summary again.
- **Design.** The most "brand-like" deck in the set. It uses a black cover with a row of diverse young faces (the target audience), the Berlingske logo, Inter/Archivo Black type, and phone mock-ups of the feature. Black and dark-grey section dividers alternate with white content slides.
- **Why it won.** The answer beats the client's revenue target in its own currency. The product feels real (mock-ups and architecture), and analogues prove the mechanism works elsewhere.

### ⑦ CBS GLOBAL 2023: Ørsted (🥇 Kosciuszko Consulting) and 2024: Novo Nordisk (🥇 Team Blessed)
- **Ørsted** (18 slides). The exec summary is a Question → Issues → Strategy → Impact grid, with two named strategies ("Eyes on the Prize" = focus on DE/NL by tender GW, and "Hydrogen Electrifies" = win auctions via wind + electrolyser system integration). There is an **alternatives table with advantages and disadvantages** for 4 system-integration options, then demand charts and an OEM partner landscape, followed by a site-map slide (central electrolyser near wind clusters). The deck shows it stays inside the DKK 200bn budget (capex 166bn), with an emissions-avoided chart, a risk heat-map and a **win-rate sensitivity table**. The design is clean white with Ørsted blue and real wind-farm photography, and a top tracker of 6 sections.
- **Novo Nordisk** (16 slides). Situation → Question → Solution → Impact, then **persona "Ellie"** with a quote and a broken patient journey. The solution, NovoJourney, has 3 numbered solutions mapped to journey stages (awareness campaign with celebrity, BetterHelp partnership, a wearables app via the acquisition of a named startup). It closes with a revenue/opex/profit schedule, a 3-year rollout, and a conclusion that ties impact to UN SDGs. The design is white with the Novo navy logo, smartwatch and app mock-ups, and many small icons. Visually the weakest of the CBS winners, which suggests the story and journey logic outweighed polish.

### ⑧ Micron BACC 2022 (Champion, Hwa Chong team) and AIYA 2022 Project Cyclo (🥇)
- **BACC.** This is an *analytics* case, so the deck mirrors the three case questions (Q1 qualitative supply-chain impacts, Q2.1 demand forecasting, Q2.2/2.3 machine capacity and LP optimisation). The team showed **failed approaches honestly** (a sinusoid baseline, an LSTM that "did not work") before the winning MLP ensemble, and listed the model's limitations. Design is a plain template with a gold/beige divider. **The repo includes the official rubric**: bands 1–10 per component, and it rewards formulation clarity, justified assumptions, "creative, feasible" ideas with "deep and elegant" evaluation, and the exact numeric LP answer. The lesson is that in analytics cases, method transparency plus a correct number beats design.
- **Project Cyclo** (10 slides, 2-person team). The structure is consultants → index → exec summary (Problems | Questions | Solutions, 3 columns) → background stats → 3-programme solution (smart hospital bins + PPE recycling + community collection) → BMC → timeline → stakeholder influence/interest grid → cost/benefit → scaling. It uses pastel colour-coded cards. This is a good template for **short (≤10-slide) international prelims**, and it targets an Indonesian (Jakarta) problem.

(Also downloaded but not analysed in depth: **IITG MnAnalyse**, which shows each synergy line with its explicit formula, DCF and NPV of synergies, stakeholder map, loyalty-migration roadmap and comparables, in a red/purple Canva style. **HCGCC 2023 GM-Tesla** is a 106-slide high-school deck, useful only as a breadth reference.)

## 3. Cross-entry patterns, and what is NEW vs `05-wijaya-team-patterns.md`

### Patterns shared by ≥5 of the 8 CBS/international winners
1. **The exec summary uses a 4-row grid** (Question / Situation or Considerations / Strategy / Impact), and it is **repeated as the closing slide** (L'Oréal, Nordea, Boozt, Berlingske, Ørsted, Novo, European Energy).
2. **The headline impact restates the client's own target and beats it** in the client's metric and currency (54% vs 50% growth; DKK 5.79M vs 5M; 104% vs 40% growth; under the DKK 200bn budget).
3. **Idea triage is shown explicitly**: impact × feasibility 2×2 of about 10 ideas (Nordea, Boozt), Harvey-ball option matrices (Berlingske, Vestas), or an advantages/disadvantages table of alternatives (Ørsted).
4. **A named persona with a journey and pain points** (Hannes, Erik, Alma, Ellie). Each tactic is pinned to the pain point it fixes, and the persona returns "after".
5. **A named strategy with 3 pillars**, often an acronym (PET, BAR, PtX), plus a recurring "map" slide that highlights the current pillar.
6. **Risk is shown as probability × impact/severity with numbered mitigations.** Every CBS winner has one.
7. **Appendix with its own TOC/navigator and numbered appendix IDs** (Appendix #1–#31). It holds a full model, sensitivity, and "why not X" slides.
8. **Section dividers are full-bleed photos.** In Invitational decks they **carry the presenter's name**, so the judges see the hand-off.

### NEW vs Wijaya patterns (adopt)
- **Beat-the-target headline.** Wijaya decks state impact but rarely frame it as "client asked for X, we deliver X+δ". Make the first impact KPI an explicit target-vs-delivered comparison.
- **Repeat the exec summary as the final slide** (a bookend for numbers, not only for the persona).
- **Pillar progress map**: one master slide with the 3–4 pillar cards, re-shown before each pillar with the active card lit (Nordea 01/02/03, Boozt BAR). This fits the Wijaya acronym style naturally.
- **Continuation titles** ("…and found that…") let consecutive slides read as one sentence. Use them for analysis chains.
- **Appendix navigator**: a TOC for the appendix split by Analysis / Finance, plus numbered IDs that the main slides cite. Wijaya already uses A1–A41. The new parts are the **navigator page** and the **Analysis vs Finance split**.
- **Per-initiative 2-variable sensitivity grids** (Nordea, L'Oréal per brand), plus a **Monte Carlo** (L'Oréal). Wijaya does one grid. Winners do one per pillar.
- **Per-market × per-channel revenue builds** in the appendix (L'Oréal 4 countries × 4 channels). This is a bottom-up model that is visibly not top-down.
- **Market-selection scoring with indices**: rank by a hard metric (NPV/MW), then overlay 3–4 contextual indices (European Energy). Use a region × criteria traffic-light table (Vestas).
- **Show the code or formula behind an index** in the appendix (European Energy sentiment function, MnAnalyse formula per synergy, BACC model). This helps a lot for data-heavy cases.
- **Working prototype on a real API** with cost per call (Boozt Gemini "Lenz") and a technical architecture slide (Berlingske). This is stronger than a Figma mock-up for tech solutions.
- **Proof-by-analogy slide**: other firms that did this and their measured results (Berlingske publishers, Nordea Moniker/Capgemini, Vestas JV precedents).
- **Regulation/compliance check slide** (EU cosmetics claims, GDPR). Wijaya does sponsor/regulation fit. The new part is a dedicated compliance-check page.
- **"Why not X" appendix slides** (Why not China, why not reviews, why still female-focused) pre-empt Q&A.
- **Presenter names on section dividers**, for live finals.
- **Show the failed approach** (BACC LSTM). In analytics cases, honest iteration earns "depth of evaluation" marks.

### Where Wijaya is already at or above this bar
- **Formula-backed numbers and assumption registers.** Wijaya matches or exceeds most winners here. Boozt 🥇 had aggressive attribution, and Novo 🥇 a thin financial schedule.
- **Stage-gated asks and capital-at-risk.** Only Vestas (gated JV pilot) and European Energy (year-by-year capital) come close. Keep doing this.
- **Branded acronyms.** These are the same as the winners' PET/BAR/PtX.

### Differences in format to note
- International finals (CBS) run **16–20 slides for GLOBAL** (virtual, short) and **30–40 main + 25–60 appendix for Invitational** (live, 4-person). Indonesian BCC finals are usually 15–25 main slides plus an appendix, which is closer to the GLOBAL format.
- CBS winners use **light backgrounds, 1 brand colour and real photography**. None use heavy illustration or comics. The Wijaya comic/persona hooks are a differentiator in Indonesia but would be unusual at CBS/HKUST-style competitions.

## 4. Good sources for the future
- **CBS Case Competition library** (https://casecompetition.com/library): the best public source found. It has official 1st/2nd/3rd decks plus case briefs for 2010–2026 (Invitational + GLOBAL), and lets you compare a podium deck against the case it solved. Also on SlideShare as `CBSCaseCompetition`.
- **Harvard Crimson Global Case Competition** (casecomp.org → Winning Teams / "Top Case Solutions 2023/24/25"): Drive links to podium decks. High-school level.
- **GitHub repo search** (`api.github.com/search/repositories?q="case competition" champion|"1st place"`). Good hits: aliencaocao/BACC-2022, rio-rsa/projectcyclo, redf-ctrl/MnAnalyse, GyaneshSamanta/Case-Competitions, tusharraju7/CaseCompetition_Decks. Unauthenticated search is rate-limited to about 10 per minute.
- **KU Case Competition Club resources** (kucasecompetitionclub.com/resources): sample decks and rubric PDFs (Wix `_files/ugd/*.pdf`).
- **CaseCrest "Exemplar/Winning Slide Examples"** (casecrest.org/resources): a PPTX of winning-slide screenshots. Needs a free account.
- **The Case Centre results pages** (Champions Trophy, Creative Shock): verify winners. Champions Trophy publishes case PDFs (`champions-trophy.squarespace.com/s/*.pdf`), not solutions.
- **Indonesia.** University news is reliable for *verification* only: feb.ugm.ac.id/en/achievement, itb.ac.id/news, feb.ui.ac.id, bbs.binus.ac.id/bbslab/competition-achieve, prasetya.ub.ac.id. LinkedIn winner posts give solution outlines (acronym, root causes) but only award photos. Occasionally a LinkedIn *document* post carries the deck (look for `feedshare-document` in the HTML), which needs a login to page through. For decks, the realistic route is to ask winners directly or attend final rounds.
