# Marketing Plan Competition Playbook

A reference for writing a marketing plan competition proposal (paper) and deck. It combines published judging rubrics, standard planning frameworks, and open-source "marketing skills" repos. Numbers in brackets point to **Sources** at the end. Lines marked *(synthesis)* are my own inference from those sources, not quotes from them.

---

## 1. What judges actually score

### 1.1 Published rubrics, side by side

| Competition | Structure / weights (as published) | Notable rules |
|---|---|---|

### 1.2 Cross-rubric takeaways *(synthesis)*
1. **Research → insight → strategy linkage** is the biggest scoring block: DECA gives 15 pts to research and insights, FFA weights primary research ×7, and VE gives SWOT 20%. Show how each finding *causes* a decision.
2. **Budget and measurement together are worth about 20–40%** (DECA 20, VE budget 20% written, BINUS folds this into "realistic"). Itemise costs and justify the split.
3. **Creativity alone scores low; creativity with feasibility scores high.** BINUS says "Kreativitas *dan* Realistis"; Brandstorm scores Innovative and Feasible/Scalable at equal weight.
4. **Q&A is heavily weighted** (FFA ×10, VE 20%). Prepare an appendix of backup slides.
5. **Format compliance costs points.** Page limits, slide counts, time limits and required bibliography are all penalised.

## 2. Planning frameworks to build the plan on

- **SOSTAC (PR Smith)**: Situation ("where are we now?": SWOT, micro-environment of customers, competitors and intermediaries, plus the macro-environment); Objectives ("where do we want to be?", numerical); Strategy ("how do we get there?": segmentation, targeting, proposition, marketing mix); Tactics; Action; Control (analytics to check whether objectives are met). The stages overlap and get revisited [19]. Use SOSTAC as the **paper skeleton**.
- **AARRR (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue)**: Corey Haines' marketing-plan skill tags every move with a funnel stage, "which makes the plan executable in priority order." Brand and content are cross-cutting rather than their own stage [1].
- **AIDA**: from E. St. Elmo Lewis (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action), a hierarchy-of-effects model [22]. Good for creative and message sequencing.
- **See-Think-Do-Care (Avinash Kaushik)**: See = the largest addressable audience; Think = actively considering; Do = ready to buy; Care = loyal customers who repurchase and advocate [20]. Use it to map **content and KPIs per stage**. It beats a bare AIDA because it includes post-purchase.
- **PESO (Gini Dietrich, 2014)**: Paid, Earned, Shared, Owned media, unified into "one measurable strategy… start with your business goals, not your media tactics" [23]. Use it as the **IMC channel-mix grid**.
- **Positioning statement (Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm*)**: "For [target customer] who [need], the [product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [primary alternative], our product [primary differentiation]." [21]
- **Competitive frame** (from the product-marketing skill): direct competitors (same solution), secondary (different solution, same problem), indirect (conflicting approach). Add JTBD Four Forces (Push, Pull, Habit, Anxiety) and verbatim customer language [2].
- **Pricing** (from the pricing skill): set price between the next-best alternative (floor) and perceived value (ceiling). Cost is a floor, never the basis, and matching a competitor's price "copies their strategy, not their economics." Use Good-Better-Best tiers and Van Westendorp's 4 questions for research [3].
- **Experiments** (from the ab-testing skill): "Because [observation/data], we believe [change] will cause [expected outcome] for [audience]. We'll know this is true when [metrics]." [4]

## 3. Recommended outline: written proposal (about 10 pages + appendix)

Map each section to the rubric line it earns points on.

1. **Cover, TOC** (not counted in the page limit at BINUS or VE [13][15]).
2. **Executive Summary (≤1 page; VE caps it at 2 [13])**: problem/opportunity, the single big idea (campaign name + tagline), 3 SMART objectives, target persona, core channel mix, total budget, and the headline KPIs and expected ROI. It must stand alone [1].
3. **Company & Product Background**: brand, portfolio, current position. Earns DECA "Knowledge of Industry/Brand" [10].
4. **Situation Analysis** (SOSTAC "S" [19]):
   - Macro: PESTEL, limited to factors that change a decision.
   - Industry and market: size, growth, trends. Indonesia context: 212M internet users (74.6% penetration) and 143M social media user identities (50.2% of population) in Jan 2025 [24].
   - Customer: secondary data plus **primary research** (survey n=?, interviews, social listening). FFA weights primary research ×7 [14].
   - Competitors: direct, secondary and indirect [2]; a perceptual map; price and channel benchmark.
   - Company: internal resources, past campaigns.
   - **SWOT → TOWS** (SO/ST/WO/WT strategies). VE weights SWOT 20% and asks *why* each item matters [13].
   - **Key problem statement + 3 insights** ("we found X, which means Y, so we will Z").
5. **Objectives**: business → marketing → communication objectives, all SMART [13], with a baseline and target, e.g. "+X% awareness among Gen Z women in Jabodetabek in 3 months." Reference them again in every later section [11].
6. **STP & Positioning**: segmentation variables (demographic, psychographic, geographic, behavioural [13]); target-selection rationale (size, accessibility, fit); 1–2 personas (goals, pains, media habits, a verbatim quote); Moore positioning statement [21]; value proposition; reasons-to-believe.
7. **Big Idea / Campaign Concept**: name, tagline, key visual, one unifying theme across all activities [11]. Optional for Brandstorm-style briefs: a sustainability or inclusivity angle [12].
8. **Marketing Mix (4P/7P)**: product tweak or bundle; price (value-based, tiers, promo mechanics without margin erosion [3]); place (Shopee/TikTok Shop/offline); promotion (summary); plus people, process and physical evidence for services.
9. **IMC & Customer Journey**: a matrix with stages (See/Think/Do/Care or AIDA) as rows and PESO channels, content type, message, CTA and KPI as columns [20][23]. Indonesia levers: TikTok and Instagram short video, live shopping, affiliates, KOL/KOC waves. Commaa's 2026 guide defines a KOC as a real customer with about 1K–10K followers. It advises blending KOLs (legitimise), influencers (scale) and KOCs (ground), and says 8–15 micro-creators usually beat one macro placement on everything except reach [25].
10. **Action Plan & Timeline**: phased (pre-launch / launch / sustain), with a Gantt chart, content calendar sample, owners and RACI [1]. Respect the competition's horizon (DECA caps campaigns at 45 days [10]).
11. **Budget**: an itemised line-item table (media, KOL fees, production, activation, tools, contingency 5–10%). State the allocation logic (objective-and-task or % split) and cost assumptions with sources. Commaa gives an indicative per-post KOL range: nano IDR 500K–2M, micro IDR 2–15M, mid-tier IDR 15–60M, macro IDR 60–200M [25]. Add projected revenue and ROI/ROAS. Corey's skill suggests 10–20% experimental budget and a 70/20/10 current/next/experimental split [1].
12. **Measurement & Control**: a KPI tree by funnel stage, a North Star metric, targets and baselines, tools (GA4, platform analytics, Shopee/TikTok seller centre, unique voucher codes, UTM links), reporting cadence, and 2–3 A/B tests in hypothesis format [4]. Contingency triggers: "if CTR < x by week 2, then reallocate…" [1].
13. **Risk & Mitigation**: brand safety, KOL scandal, stock-outs, Komdigi disclosure compliance [25], budget overrun [17].
14. **Conclusion**: restate the objective-to-KPI link.
15. **References** (DECA requires a bibliography [10]) and **Appendix**: questionnaire, raw survey charts, mock-ups, detailed calculations.

### Funnel metric cheat-sheet [26]
- ROAS = revenue attributed to ads / ad spend. Break-even ROAS = 1 / gross margin %, so a 40% margin means 2.5×.
- Simple CAC = total marketing spend / new customers. "Fully loaded" CAC adds agency, creative, tools and salaries [26][1].
- MER = total revenue / total paid marketing spend.
- LTV = AOV × purchase frequency × lifespan. Gross-profit LTV = LTV × margin. Target LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 as a widely cited floor [26].
- Also show CTR, CPM/CPC, engagement rate, conversion rate (visit → add-to-cart → purchase), repeat purchase rate, and brand lift (pre/post survey).
- Rate hygiene: only divide closed observation windows. Label in-window cohorts as pending [1].

## 4. Recommended deck outline (about 15–20 slides; Brandstorm round 1 = 3 slides)

The DECA order is a safe default [10]:
1. Cover (campaign name + key visual)
2. Agenda
3. **Overview / Opportunity**: problem in one sentence + the size of the prize
4. Brand & industry snapshot
5. **Research method & key insights** (primary + secondary; 3 insight cards)
6. SWOT/TOWS → key problem statement
7. SMART objectives (baseline → target)
8. Target persona(s) + STP rationale
9. Positioning statement + value proposition
10. **Big idea** (theme, tagline, key visual)
11. Customer journey × channel matrix (STDC/AIDA × PESO)
12. Hero tactics, 2–3 slides with mock-ups (TikTok script/storyboard, Shopee campaign page, KOL wave plan, offline activation)
13. Timeline / Gantt + content calendar excerpt
14. Budget (pie + table) + ROI/ROAS projection
15. KPI dashboard by funnel stage + measurement tools
16. Risks & contingency
17. Closing: the objective → idea → KPI chain on one slide
18. References; then appendix backup slides for Q&A (calculations, survey data, alternative scenarios)

Deck craft:
- Use action titles (every slide headline states the takeaway).
- Mark numbers you haven't verified as placeholders and collect them in a list, as the emotixco pitch-deck skill does [6].
- Every member presents (DECA requires it [10]).
- Rehearse to time. VE and FFA penalise overruns [13][14].

## 5. Common judge criticisms (and fixes)

*(Synthesis: inverted from the rubric criteria above and from the "failure modes" list in Corey Haines' marketing-plan skill [1].)*

| Criticism | Rubric hook | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Analysis is a data dump; no insight." | DECA research → "how findings inform" [10]; MBS "supported with facts" [17] | End every analysis block with a "so what" and link it to a specific tactic. |
| "No primary research" / survey too thin | FFA primary research ×7 [14] | Run a quick survey (state n and sampling), 5–10 interviews, and social listening. Put the questionnaire in the appendix. |
| "Objectives are vague or not SMART; disconnected from KPIs." | VE SMART [13]; DECA "referenced throughout" [11] | Give each objective a baseline, target, deadline and owner KPI, and repeat it in the measurement section. |
| "Target market too broad ('18–35 all Indonesia')." | VE segmentation [13] | Name one primary persona with psychographics and media habits, and give a rationale for choosing it. |
| "Creative but unrealistic" / "realistic but boring" | BINUS Creativity & Realism 20% [15]; Brandstorm Innovative + Feasible [12] | One bold big idea, executed through proven low-risk channels. Show feasibility evidence (cost quotes, precedents). |
| "Tactic list with no unifying theme or sequencing." | DECA unifying theme [11]; [1] "listing tactics without sequencing" | One campaign concept, phased timeline, each tactic tagged with a funnel stage. |
| "Budget unjustified, round numbers, missing costs." | DECA "all costs… considered" [11]; VE "reasoning for allocation" [13] | Itemised unit cost × quantity with sources, contingency, and ROI/ROAS/CAC math. |
| "KPIs are vanity (likes/views only)." | DECA Impact & Measurement [10] | Use a full-funnel KPI tree that reaches sales, CAC, ROAS and retention, with tracking tools named. |
| "Ignores the client's constraints or the brief." | [1] "recommending things the team can't execute"; Brandstorm scope [12] | Re-read the case and restate the client's ask on slide 3. Fit within the stated budget and time horizon. |
| "Inconsistent answers in Q&A; numbers don't add up." | MBS "remained consistent" [17]; FFA Q&A ×10 [14] | Prepare backup slides and one master spreadsheet. Run a rehearsal Q&A with every member. |
| "Format violations" (page/slide count, missing bibliography, overtime) | DECA penalty checklist [10]; VE penalties [13] | Run a compliance checklist before submission. |
| "Uncomfortable facts glossed over" (e.g. churn, low margin) | [1] failure modes | Name them as risks or open decisions, each with a mitigation. |

## 6. Useful open-source skill repos (what each is good for)

- **coreyhaines31/marketingskills**: a large library (ab-testing, ads, pricing, product-marketing, marketing-plan, launch, competitors, influencer-marketing, social…). The **marketing-plan** skill gives a 13-section AARRR plan. Its quality bar: every move tagged by stage, owners on the roadmap, the exec summary standing alone, explicit open decisions, and skipped ideas explained [1][5].
- **emotixco/claude-skills-founder**: competitor matrix (5–8 competitors, sourced prices, positioning gaps), personas (3, flagged as hypotheses, with a priority matrix), pricing-strategy (6 models, 3 tiers, anchoring/decoy), go-to-market (90-day plan), pitch-deck (12 slides with action headlines). Its philosophy is "Specific / Opinionated / Sourced"; it says "Estimate" or "Not found" rather than guessing [6].
- **ericosiu/ai-marketing-skills**: workflow-style skills (growth experiment engine, content ops with scoring rubrics/expert panel, conversion ops CRO audit, deck generator) [7]. Use its rubric idea to self-score a draft before submitting.
- **EdoStra/Marketing-for-Founders**: a curated link list by channel (launch sites, social, SEO, email, ads, influencer, pricing, positioning, CRO, user research) [8].
- **ronakganatra/awesome-marketing**: a curated list (books, case studies, strategy & growth, tools) [9].
