# 04 — Deck, Storytelling & Pitch/Q&A Playbook (Case Competitions)

A working reference for building a consulting-style deck (McKinsey/BCG/Bain conventions), telling the story, presenting it and getting through judge Q&A. Claims are tied to sources in brackets, e.g. [S3], which are listed at the end. Lines marked *(synthesis)* are my own combination of several sources.

---

## 1. Core logic: the Pyramid Principle (Minto)

- **The idea.** Group low-level facts that are alike, draw an insight from them, then group those insights into higher ones, until you end up with one pyramid held together by a single thought. To communicate it, walk the reader down the pyramid. Minto's own site calls it the de facto standard at major consulting firms. [S1]
- **Top-down rules** [S2]:
  1. A point at any level summarises the points grouped below it.
  2. Every point in a group is the same kind of idea.
  3. Each group follows a logical order: deductive, chronological, structural, or comparative (by importance).
- **Vertical logic (Q&A dialogue).** Each statement raises a question in the reader's mind (Why? How?), and the line below answers it. Don't raise a question before you're ready to answer it, and don't answer one nobody has asked yet. A section called "Our Assumptions" placed before the main points is the classic mistake. [S3]
- **Horizontal logic (within a group).** A group argues either **inductively** (parallel items that share a plural noun, such as reasons or steps) or **deductively** (a chain ending in "therefore"), never both at once. [S3][S4]
- **MECE.** Groupings should be mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive. Inductive groups should hold fewer than ~5 items. [S2][S4]
- **Summary points must not be "intellectually blank."** Write "He did it because X", not "He did it for three reasons." [S2]
- **The apex must be a claim** that someone could disagree with, not a topic. "Options for the northern site" is a topic. "Allocate the northern site and drop the extension" is a claim. [S5]
- **Two ways to build it** [S2]: *top-down* (write the Q&A first, then the Situation and Complication) or *bottom-up* (list every point, work out cause and effect, then draw conclusions).

### SCQA / SCR introduction
- **Situation** (what the reader already accepts) → **Complication** (what changed) → **Question** (what that forces us to decide) → **Answer** (the governing thought). [S3][S4]
- Orderings Minto lists [S2]: Standard S-C-A, Direct A-S-C, Concerned C-S-A, Aggressive Q-S-C.
- Use **S-C-R** when the audience may resist the answer. Use **R-S-C** when they'll mostly accept it but still need conviction. [S13]
- Where the "problem" goes depends on what the audience already knows. If they know the problem but not its root causes, put the problem in S and the root causes plus impact in C. [S13]
- Three framings of the same conclusion: neutral, **springboard** (positive opportunity), and **burning platform** (negative). [S11]
- A pattern you can reuse: "S + C + C + C + R". Back up each complication with its evidence before you introduce the next one. [S12]
- A strong resolution says what will be done, how, when and by whom, with milestones, side benefits and expected consequences. [S13]

## 2. Action titles, ghost deck, horizontal/vertical deck logic

- **Action title:** a full-sentence claim, not a label. "Revenue by segment" is a label. "Enterprise now drives 60% of revenue but less than half of new logos" is an action title. [S6][S7]
- **Read-through test:** read only the titles from top to bottom. If they still argue the case, the deck works. If you get a list of nouns, you have a table of contents pretending to be an argument. [S6][S8]
- **Deck vertical logic:** everything on a slide supports that slide's title and nothing else. **Deck horizontal logic:** the titles in sequence form the storyline. [S6][S7]
- **Three title levels:** deck title (the recommendation or question), **section tracker** (short labels like "Where we are today", which may be labels), and an action title on every content slide. Don't blur them. [S8]
- **Title failure modes:** the *hedged* title ("Churn may potentially be influenced…"; instead say "Pricing explains roughly half the churn increase") and the *overreaching* title, which claims more than the chart shows. [S16]
- **Ghost deck (also called storyboard, skeleton, wireframe, dot-dash):** empty pages that carry only an action title and a rough sketch of the exhibit. Pressure-test the argument at this stage, because reordering 15 titles takes minutes and reordering 15 finished slides takes an afternoon. [S6][S7]
- **Working sequence** [S7]: (1) write the governing thought in one sentence; (2) frame SCQA, which becomes the executive summary; (3) draft 8–15 action titles; (4) check horizontal logic and MECE; (5) make each slide vertically consistent; (6) design last, and quietly.
- **Dense vs. cluttered:** density means a lot of information serving one point. Clutter means several points competing. [S7]

## 3. Deck architecture

A typical consulting deck has five parts: **front page → executive summary → body → recommendation/next steps → appendix.** The appendix is often longer than the main deck. [S9][S10]

- **The body maps one-to-one** onto the supporting points in the executive summary. [S10b]
- **Case-competition shape** [S14]: exec summary (slide 1) → situation/analysis → recommendation → implementation (timeline, resources, costs) → risks & mitigation → backup slides.
- **If the case has several issues,** give the conclusion for each issue and then summarise all the solutions on one slide. [S15]

### Executive summary slide checklist
- [ ] The title states the answer and its main benefit. Never use "Executive summary" as the headline. [S17]
- [ ] It carries the **governing thought**: one sentence saying what the audience should believe or do. [S18]
- [ ] It has 3–5 MECE supporting points, one reason each. [S17][S18]
- [ ] It ends with the decision or ask: what to approve, and by when. [S17]
- [ ] Every figure traces to a body slide or source, with a source line that names the slides (e.g., "slides 5–11"). [S17]
- [ ] It follows SCR. MBB executive summaries usually do. [S9]
- [ ] It can stand alone: a judge who reads only this slide knows your full answer. [S14][S9]
- [ ] It isn't an agenda in disguise ("We will cover the market and options"). [S16]
- [ ] It doesn't hedge every point, which hides which findings are firm. [S17]
- [ ] Bolded lead sentences carry the storyline on their own (BCG example). [S11]

### Appendix strategy
- The appendix is where your homework goes. The slide is where your conclusion goes. [S6]
- Put methodology, models, sensitivity tables, cohort cuts, survey tabs and interview quotes there. Reference them from body footnotes ("See A-14 for full cohort cut"). [S18]
- **Answer verbally first,** then pull up a backup slide if needed. [S19] Smoothly bringing up a backup slide is "one of the most impressive things a team can do." [S14]
- Make an **appendix index slide** that hyperlinks to each backup and shows during Q&A, plus a progress bar with a link back to the appendix. [S20]
- Include every calculation and its variables in the appendix. [S15]
- Don't walk through the appendix during the talk. It burns time on non-decisions. [S18]

## 4. Slide design rules (checklist)

- [ ] **One message per slide.** If a page makes two points, make it two pages. [S6][S10b]
- [ ] An action title of ≤2 lines that states the "so what" *(synthesis)*.
- [ ] One exhibit that proves the title. Move other data to the appendix. [S16]
- [ ] **A source line on every data slide** (origin, year, "team analysis" if you transformed the data) and footnotes for definitions. [S16][S9]
- [ ] Slide number at bottom right and source at bottom left. [S18][S9]
- [ ] **Tracker / navigation bar** that shows the current section. Judges then know where you are and where you've been. [S20][S21]
- [ ] Muted palette with **one accent colour** used only on the number that matters. [S6][S10b]
- [ ] One typeface in 2–3 sizes, and real white space. [S10b]
- [ ] No 3D, heavy gridlines, drop shadows or rainbow charts. Label data directly and drop the legend. [S6][S16]
- [ ] Few or no animations. [S9][S21] Export to **PDF** to avoid formatting drift on the venue PC, and split any animated builds into a sequence of slides. [S20]
- [ ] Build a template before the competition with fonts, palette and icons. Avoid yellow and unprofessional fonts. [S15][S21]
- [ ] No data dumps. "If you need notes, your slides are too complicated!" [S21]
- [ ] Looks matter. Judges' first impressions are visual. [S22]

### Chart selection cheat-sheet [S23][S24][S25]
| Message / question | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| How is this changing over time? | Line (many periods); column (few) | Pie |
| How big is A vs B? | Horizontal bar, sorted | Pie, 3D bars |
| What is it made of? | Stacked / 100% stacked bar; pie only for 3–5 slices | Pie with 6+ slices or slices under 5% |
| Size **and** mix at once (market map) | Mekko / Marimekko | Several separate charts |
| How did we get from A to B? | Waterfall / bridge (EBITDA walk, variance) | Grouped bar |
| How do variables relate? | Scatter / bubble / 2×2 | Dual axes |
| Distribution | Histogram, box plot | — |
| Sensitivity | Tornado | Waterfall |
| Plan / timeline | Gantt | Bullet list |
| Qualitative scoring | Harvey balls | Arbitrary numeric scores |
| Before → after across items | Dumbbell / slopegraph | — |

Start bars at zero. Position and length beat angle and area for accurate comparison. [S25] Check that each chart's message supports the overall narrative, or no reformatting will help. [S26]

## 5. Template ghost deck — 14-slide case deck (+ appendix)

Replace the `[…]` placeholders. Every title is a claim. The tracker sections are **Context | Diagnosis | Recommendation | Execution**. *(Structure is a synthesis of [S7][S10b][S14][S13].)*

| # | Tracker | Action title (template) | Exhibit sketch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | **Cover:** "[Client] should [do X] to [reach goal] by [year]" | Team, date, logo |
| 2 | — | **Exec summary:** "[Do X] delivers [+$Y / Z%] by [year] with [manageable risk]" | SCR in 3 bolded lines + 3 support points + ask |
| 3 | Context | "[Client] has [won with A], but [metric] has [fallen/stalled] since [year]" | Line chart of the KPI vs. peers |
| 4 | Context | "Unless addressed, the gap widens to [$Y] by [year]" (burning platform) | Base-case projection / waterfall |
| 5 | Diagnosis | "[Root cause 1] explains ~[x]% of the gap" | Waterfall / driver tree |
| 6 | Diagnosis | "[Segment/market] is the most attractive pool: [size, growth, fit]" | Mekko or bubble chart |
| 7 | Diagnosis | "[Client]'s [capability] gives it a right to win vs. [competitors]" | Harvey-ball competitor table |
| 8 | Recommendation | "We evaluated [3] options; [Option B] best meets [criteria]" | Options × criteria matrix |
| 9 | Recommendation | "[Option B] means [3 concrete initiatives]" | 3-column initiative cards |
| 10 | Recommendation | "The plan adds [$NPV / revenue] at [x]% IRR, with payback in [n] months" | Revenue/cost bars + key assumptions box |
| 11 | Execution | "A phased rollout de-risks the plan: pilot → scale → [expand]" | Gantt with go/no-go gates and owners |
| 12 | Execution | "[n] KPIs track success; expansion is gated on [threshold]" | KPI dashboard table |
| 13 | Execution | "Top risks ([A], [B], [C]) are mitigable" | Probability × impact matrix + mitigations |
| 14 | — | **Close:** "We ask [client] to approve [X] and [first step] by [date]" | Recap of the 3 points + ask |
| A0 | Appendix | Appendix index (hyperlinked) | Linked tiles [S20] |
| A1–An | Appendix | Market sizing; financial model and assumptions; sensitivity (tornado); options scoring detail; rejected alternatives; competitor benchmarks; implementation cost build; survey/interview evidence | — |

**Ghost deck QA:**
- [ ] Do the titles alone (slides 2–14) read as one argument? [S6]
- [ ] Do the diagnosis slides cover the causes MECE? [S2]
- [ ] Does each recommendation slide map to a support point on slide 2? [S10b]
- [ ] Does every number on slide 2 appear on a body slide? [S17]
- [ ] Is there a risk slide and an implementation slide with timeline, resources and cost? [S14]
- [ ] Is there one deep recommendation rather than several shallow ones? [S14][S22]

## 6. Delivery checklist (case competitions)

- [ ] **Preparation:** spend the first 15–20 minutes reading the case and agreeing on the core question before building any slides. [S14]
- [ ] Answer the prompt you were given and avoid scope creep. Make the solution tangible, actionable and within a believable budget. [S14][S22]
- [ ] Set a hard stop time for building slides and keep to it. [S21]
- [ ] Time every full run. **Going over time is one of the fastest ways to lose points.** [S14]
- [ ] Plan speaker handoffs. Smooth transitions signal teamwork. [S14]
- [ ] Know the content cold, without notes. [S21]
- [ ] Open strongly. Light a fire in the first minute with PIP: purpose + importance + preview. [S13]
- [ ] Don't recreate your discovery process chronologically. Lead with the answer (unless the audience is hostile). [S13]
- [ ] Close by repeating the recommendation and action program: who, time, cost, next steps. [S13]
- [ ] Use stakeholder and customer examples to keep judges engaged. [S22]
- [ ] Mind your presence: posture while not speaking, and greet the judges. [S15]
- [ ] Know every number and **how you got it**. [S22]

## 7. Judge Q&A playbook

**Mindset.** Q&A is often the tiebreaker between closely ranked teams. [S14] Treat questions as chances to strengthen the recommendation, not only to defend it. [S19]

**Process: LPTAC.** Listen, Pause, Think, Answer, Confirm. Never interrupt. Work out why the judge is asking before you answer. "Did you consider outsourcing?" may really be about capability, cost or speed. [S19]

**Answer shape: Answer → Evidence → Implication, then stop.** Begin with yes, no, a number or a position. Don't open with background, repeated thanks or a framework definition. [S19]

**Question types and how to handle them** [S19]:
- *Clarification:* answer directly without re-presenting.
- *Assumption testing:* give the source, your confidence, the sensitivity and how you'd validate it.
- *Trade-off:* acknowledge it and explain why it's acceptable.
- *Alternative perspective (CFO, COO, regulator):* answer through their lens.
- *Alternative recommendation:* compare against your decision criteria, and don't dismiss a credible alternative.
- *Loaded premise* ("Wouldn't acquisition obviously be faster?"): you don't have to accept it.
- *Financial:* give the number, the calculation or assumption, the sensitivity or threshold, and what it means for the decision. Never invent a precise figure.

**"It depends"** is fine if you finish it: say what it depends on, the threshold, and which decision follows. [S19]

**"I don't know" structure:** what we know, what we can reasonably infer, what we'd learn (and how), and how the decision is protected (e.g., expansion gated on a threshold). Don't answer everything with "more research." Say what's missing, why it matters, how you'd collect it and what decision it would change. [S19] An alternative: give preliminary thoughts, both positive and negative, and commit to more work. [S27]

**Team rules:**
- One primary responder per question. [S15][S19]
- Add to a teammate's answer only when it adds real value, and never contradict the presentation or a teammate unnecessarily. [S19]
- Disagree with judges respectfully, and correct weak answers briefly. [S19]

**Bridging.** Answer the question asked, then connect back to the recommendation (that's the "Implication" step). Don't detour first ("Before answering, let me explain…"), because the judge will feel unheard. [S19]

**Preparation:**
- [ ] Write **20+ tough questions** and practise answering them aloud. [S14]
- [ ] Practise difficult, repeated, ambiguous and follow-up questions, including one "I don't know." [S19]
- [ ] Assign an owner to each question area *(synthesis)*.
- [ ] Map each likely question to a backup slide number *(synthesis)*.

### 30 likely judge questions
*Questions 1–13 and 25–29 come from or paraphrase [S19][S27][S14]. The rest are a synthesis based on the deck structure above.*
1. What is your single most important assumption, and what if it's wrong?
2. Why did you assume [x]% conversion / adoption / market share?
3. How confident are you in the market size? What's your source?
4. What must be true for this recommendation to work?
5. Does the plan still work if adoption is 20% below your base case? (sensitivity)
6. What is your greatest implementation risk?
7. What are you choosing *not* to do? What does this sacrifice?
8. Why not acquire / build internally / partner instead?
9. Did you consider outsourcing?
10. Doesn't the partnership create too much dependency?
11. What exactly does the [$X] investment include?
12. Which customer segment goes first, and is this a pilot or a full launch?
13. Who owns implementation, and what happens if a readiness gate is missed?
14. What's the payback period / NPV / IRR, and what discount rate did you use?
15. How is the plan funded, and what does it do to cash flow and the balance sheet?
16. How will competitors respond, and how fast?
17. What is the client's right to win? Why can't a competitor copy this?
18. What KPIs tell you in 6 months whether this is working?
19. What's your kill or pivot criterion?
20. What's the quick win in the first 90 days?
21. What are the regulatory, legal or ESG implications?
22. How does this affect employees, customers and other stakeholders?
23. Does the organisation have the capabilities and talent to execute?
24. How does this fit the client's stated strategy and constraints in the case?
25. How do your revenue growth assumptions differ from history and from consensus? [S27]
26. What are the strongest opposing views that could prove your forecast wrong? [S27]
27. What are the company's constraints to growth? [S27]
28. Do you expect management to change its past style (e.g., organic vs. M&A growth)? [S27]
29. What's your assessment of the competitive environment (e.g., Five Forces)? [S27]
30. If you could only do one thing from this deck, what would it be?
