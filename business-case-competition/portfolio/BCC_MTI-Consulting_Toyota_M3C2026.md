## Storyline / argument pattern
- **Hook:** "a cost gap that is widening, not closing". The gap is quantified against a moving benchmark (106 vs 89 → 58% by 2030). The team also explains why selling more cars won't fix it: capacity utilization is already 70%.
- **Symptoms → root cause:** 6 connected problems reduce to an issue tree with **2 root causes**. The framing line: "The cost gap is the symptom; disconnected systems and an underused workforce are the cause." Evidence such as the 31% alert-override rate shows that adding technology alone fails.
- **Solution naming:** **FLOW!** is an acronym that also carries meaning: it extends Toyota's own "flow" concept and maps one letter to each root cause and pillar (Flexible Connect Backbone / Loop-Closed Jidoka / Operators as Owners / Wisdom Capture). Pillar names reuse client vocabulary (Jidoka, kaizen, one-point lessons).
- **Closed loop:** the four pillars form one improvement cycle (F data → L detect → O fix → W standardize → back to F). One recurring concrete story, "one missed sealer bead", is used in the body, the appendix storyboard and the prototypes.
- **Human-centered guardrails as taglines:** "AI points, people decide", "Reuse before buying", "Built by TMMIN's people", "No layoffs".
- **Impact:** Rp43bn invested, Rp45bn/yr benefit, NPV +Rp44bn at 10%, payback 2029, peak exposure ~Rp10bn, index 106→~99 (net ~100). The team is honest that "FLOW! alone does not close the gap; it stops it from widening."
- **Close:** a small, reversible first step (Phase 0 in Q4 2026: validate costs with plant finance, complete camera validation, baseline a model line and a comparison line, announce the no-layoff commitment with the union).

## Frameworks & analyses used
Issue tree (MECE); SMART key question; maturity-level gap (levels 1–4); PESTEL; Porter's Five Forces; SWOT → TOWS mapped to pillars; problem-to-pillar matrix; weighted-criteria option scoring with sensitivity to the weights; impact–feasibility prioritization; phased roadmap with stage gates; stakeholder power–interest; RACI; risk heat map and register; ADKAR (semifinal); KPI tree with leading and lagging KPIs; pilot with a control line (quasi-experimental); assumption register tied to gate tests; NPV/payback/ROI; scenario and stress tests; break-even % of benefits; cost-index bridge (waterfall); personas (semifinal); prototype mock-ups.

## How numbers are handled
- **Sources:** casebook exhibits are cited inline ("Exhibit 3", "Exhibit 9") alongside APA-style external references (GAIKINDO, McKinsey Brink et al. 2021, Bristol et al. 2024, BCG 2024, RM.id, Gotoh 2024, ISA, Indonesian laws). Every figure has a "Source:" line.
- **Lever-level valuation (Fig 13):** for each lever: KPI moved → formula in rupiah (e.g., "8% × Rp560bn × 30% = Rp13.5bn → 13") → amount → how it will be checked at which gate. The total is Rp45bn = Rp37bn savings (6.6% of conversion cost) + Rp8bn avoided capex.
- **Conservatism is stated explicitly:** rates sit below published best cases (30% vs 49–68% scrap cuts; 20% vs 18–25% maintenance); only half of freed operator time is counted; figures are rounded down; each saving goes through one cost bucket (double-counting checks).
- **Risk framing:** downside/base/upside cards (NPV +4.1 / +44.4 / +68.6bn); break-even at ~45% of benefits; 9 of 10 stress tests positive; only Rp17bn committed before Gate 1; "money follows verified savings".
- **Team estimates are labelled** ("team estimate", "team assumption", assumption IDs such as A4, A10, A11) and will be replaced by vendor quotes or plant finance data in Phase 0.
- **Plausibility checks:** Rp211k saved per car vs a Rp3.2m conversion cost, and the result only returns the index to its 2023 level.

## Strengths
- A very tight causal chain: 6 problems → 2 root causes → 4 pillars → KPIs → rupiah, with explicit mapping tables at every step (problem-to-pillar, TOWS-to-pillar, KPI tree).
- Financial defensibility: transparent formulas, below-benchmark rates, a double-counting check, break-even %, stage-gated capital at risk, and an honest admission that FLOW! does not fully close the gap.
- Strong case-specific detail: client vocabulary, the existing CCTV AI camera, AKTI, the union, and one repeated concrete story (the sealer bead).
- Human-centered framing (no layoffs, AI suggests and people decide) addresses the case's emphasis on people.
- Prototype screens and personas make the idea tangible.
- Action-titled captions mean the figure list alone reads as the argument.

## Weaknesses / what a judge would criticize
- Length and density: 43→64 pages and very wordy justified body text. Readers may skim, and the semifinal main body doubled to 15 pages.
- Heavy dependence on team assumptions for the cost split (scrap 8%, maintenance 12%, labor 40%). The team acknowledges this, but it remains the soft spot.
- The NPV window is short (2026–2030) with a modest net multiple (1.5× net), and the downside NPV is only +Rp4bn.
- Framework density (PESTEL, Five Forces, SWOT, TOWS, ADKAR, RACI…) risks a "framework dump" impression in the appendix, though most are tied to the pillars.
- Single-font design is clean but plain. Charts are many and similar in style, so the hierarchy between key exhibits and backup is weak.
- Minor: a font change between rounds (Liberation → Arial) suggests a different toolchain, which is harmless but shows the rebuild.

## Preliminary → Semifinal: what changed when they advanced
1. **The document became a full proposal:** "Business Executive Summary" → "Business Proposal"; 43 → 64 pages; main body 7 → 15 pages; body figures 7 → 17; ~8.3k → ~15.1k words; references 1 → 2 pages.
2. **Core numbers frozen:** Rp43bn, Rp45bn/yr, NPV +Rp44bn, payback 2029 and index 106→99 are unchanged. The semifinal says so explicitly: "The numbers are those of our preliminary submission; the reasoning and tests are new." The upgrade was defensibility, not new math.
3. **Chapters restructured to mirror a rubric:**
   - Prelim: I Problem, II Strategic Recommendation, III Implementation Direction, IV Expected Business Impact.
   - Semi: I Business & Problem, II Proposed Solution, III Value Proposition, IV Implementation Roadmap, V Feasibility, VI Impact & Measurement, VII Conclusion.
   - Value Proposition and Feasibility became standalone chapters.
4. **Each pillar expanded** from one paragraph into a templated subsection (*What it fixes / How it works / Who and with what / Target*) with its own figure.
5. **New credibility layers:**
   - Feasibility scorecard (4 dimensions) with external evidence: Toyota AI platform (~1,200 workers), AKTI (429 graduates, 6,297 applicants for 64 seats), ISA/IEC 62443, UU PDP, Perpres 79/2023, plus a new Appendix H.
   - "What competitors cannot copy" with 3 differentiators (AI learns from overrides, every fix becomes a standard automatically, money follows verified savings).
   - Value for each stakeholder group, plus personas.
   - Resourcing: people needed per phase, no new permanent hires, FTE ranges.
   - Capex/opex split and depreciation; ADKAR change management; gate decision table.
6. **Financial section rewritten as Q&A:** "Where does every rupiah come from? / What if we are wrong? / Is Rp45bn realistic?". It adds the lever valuation table, downside/base/upside cards, 10 stress tests, a break-even curve, cost-share ±2pt sensitivity, a maintenance-reach test and six plausibility checks. It reads as pre-empting judges' Q&A questions from the preliminary round.
7. **KPIs:** 7 → 8 headline KPIs, split into 3 leading and 5 lagging, with a review cadence (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly).
8. **Honesty upgrades:** the net index is stated as ~100 after depreciation (the prelim footnoted it), there is an explicit "FLOW! alone does not close the gap", and the camera fallback plan is spelled out if validation fails.
9. **Visual system unchanged** (same cover, header, palette and components), so continuity and brand recognition carry across rounds.

**Takeaway:** advancing was handled by keeping the core numbers and design locked and adding layers that answer likely judge objections: feasibility, defensibility, people and resourcing, and robustness of the numbers.

## Reusable patterns
- Name the solution as an acronym that (a) extends a concept the client already owns and (b) maps one letter to each root cause; put the letters on the cover as a tagline.
- Reduce many symptoms to **2 root causes** with an issue tree, and write one line saying "X is the symptom; Y and Z are the cause."
- Write a SMART key question with numeric baseline→target, a deadline and a budget cap, then say why it is SMART.
- Use a fixed per-pillar template: What it fixes / How it works / Who & with what / Target (baseline→2030).
- Use one recurring concrete incident ("one missed sealer bead") across body, appendix storyboard and prototypes.
- Use a lever valuation table: Lever | KPI baseline→target | formula to rupiah | Rp/yr | how and when it's checked (gate).
- Use three scenario cards (downside/base/upside) with assumptions, NPV/payback/peak exposure and a "What management does" footer.
- Show a break-even % of planned benefits plus a stress-test list, and state capital at risk before Gate 1.
- Use stage gates where the next tranche is released only after finance confirms savings ("money follows proof").
- Write captions as action titles so the list of figures reads as the argument.
- Put a one-page infographic exec summary in Appendix A (Situation / Problem & root causes / Solution / Implementation / Impact, with big-number tiles).
- Add a "What competitors cannot copy" paragraph and a "Value for each group" figure.
- Add a Feasibility chapter scored on Technical / Organizational / Financial / Regulatory with cited evidence.
- When advancing a round, keep the numbers and design fixed and add defensibility; say explicitly "numbers unchanged, reasoning and tests new."
- Close with a small, reversible Phase 0 first step.
