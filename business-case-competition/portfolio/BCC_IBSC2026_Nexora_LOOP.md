## Storyline / argument pattern
- **Hook / reframe:** "Nexora now earns more *from* its users than *with* them". About 80% of the 15T revenue rise came from fees, not growth. Urgency comes from a projection: Scrolla's GMV passes Nexora's within ~3 years.
- **Problem → root cause:** 4 linked problems (P1–P4) drawn as a vicious cycle, then 4 controllable root causes (RC1 uniform fees, RC2 rule-based incentives, RC3 no content engine, RC4 unoptimised routing). Each root cause has a confidence rating and the pilot test that would confirm it.
- **Solution naming:** **LOOP** = Leverage the Logistics Moat / Optimize Seller Economics / Own the Buyer Loyalty / Power MSME Growth. The name doubles as the mechanism, a self-funding growth loop (Earn → Share → Grow). The title "From Extraction to Gain-Sharing" frames it as a positioning shift.
- **Signature guardrail:** a **"50% Profit Rule"**: incentives never exceed half of the savings earned that year. This answers the case constraint that profit must grow every year.
- **Alternatives are steelmanned:** raise fees (the Commercial Director's view), bring back subsidies, copy Scrolla. The fee hike is shown to be close on 5-year NPV but to lose once terminal value is counted.
- **Impact:** Year-2 profit 11.1T vs 7.4T status quo; NPV 10.1T at 13%; capex 1.2T repaid in ~14 months; profit grows in all scenarios.
- **Honest caveat:** market share still falls (46%→44.4% Y2) and the team explains why a share target was rejected.

## Frameworks & analyses used
Revenue decomposition (price vs volume); operating-profit bridge; competitor benchmarking; problem severity matrix; MECE issue tree with evidence, confidence and test; SMART objectives table; weighted alternative scoring (5 criteria); business-model loop (sources and uses of a Growth Fund); governance decision rights (proposes/approves/rule); unit economics and break-even churn; uplift-modelling segments (persuadables / sure things / lost causes / do-not-disturb); adoption funnel; AI architecture with failure modes; assumption register with confidence; 5-year P&L bridge with formulas; incremental cash flow, NPV and sensitivity to the discount rate; scenarios plus a tornado; terminal-value comparison; stage gates with holdout/control groups at 95% confidence; risk matrix; KPI dashboard with scale/stop rules.

## How numbers are handled
- **Case facts vs assumptions are separated:** the assumption register tags each item "Case fact / Assumption / Policy" with source, Year-2 profit impact (±) and a confidence level.
- **Formulas are printed** (e.g., Volume contribution = (GMV − 600) × 10% × 25%; Capacity cost = parcels × (share − 50%) × [1,000 + 2,000 × (share − 50%)]).
- **Reconciliation notes:** the revenue decomposition sums to 15.2T instead of 15T, and the team explains the rounding (2024 take rate 8.04%).
- **Incremental logic:** only the difference vs the status quo counts (the status quo also shifts parcels in-house).
- **Discount rate is built and justified:** 13% = 7.64% bond yield + 5.4pp premium, with NPV shown at 11%/15%. IRR is flagged as "not meaningful", with the reason given.
- **Benchmarks are labelled "directional evidence, not Nexora forecasts"** (Shopee ads +70%, VIP spend +30–40%, Booking.com uplift study).
- Every exhibit has a "Source: Casebook / Team Analysis" line, and the body cites appendices inline.

## Strengths
- Very efficient 3-page main body: every pillar gets a one-line outcome, a mechanism and a three-number chip row tied to a root cause.
- One memorable mechanism (a self-funding loop under the 50% Profit Rule) that directly answers the case constraint.
- Rigorous, reconciled financials: formulas, an incremental vs status-quo view, a discount-rate build, terminal value, a tornado and a confidence-tagged assumption register.
- The opposing view (fee hike) is steelmanned with numbers rather than dismissed.
- Built-in experimentation (10% holdouts, 95% confidence gates, scale/stop rules), plus AI governance with failure modes and regulatory awareness (KPPU, UU PDP).

## Weaknesses / what a judge would criticize
- Extremely dense, and the many abbreviations (SQ, RC1–4, G1–G3, T for trillion) increase cognitive load. Font sizes in chips and exhibits are small.
- The exec summary sits after the references (p6) rather than up front. Depending on the rules this is fine, but a judge flipping pages may miss it.
- Share still declines under LOOP; some judges may want a growth/share answer to the Scrolla threat.
- The biggest value driver (2.0T marketing savings from uplift targeting) is "medium-low" confidence by the team's own rating. The structure is honest, but it is a target for Q&A.
- Mixing serif body with a sans UI looks academic in the body text compared with the polished cards.
- The result is unknown, possibly not advanced: the depth may have exceeded what a preliminary screener rewards, compared with clarity and a punchy narrative.

## Reusable patterns
- Reframe the problem in one antithesis line ("earns more *from* its users than *with* them") and use the inverse as the solution title ("From Extraction to Gain-Sharing").
- Draw the problems as a vicious cycle and answer with a virtuous loop. The name of the solution (LOOP) is the mechanism.
- Use a KPI chip row under each pillar: [Fixes RC#] [baseline→target] [unit metric] [rupiah impact].
- Add a named financial guardrail rule (e.g., "50% Profit Rule") that makes the plan self-funding and enforceable.
- Use a root-cause table with Evidence / Confidence / How the pilot tests it columns.
- Start every appendix page with a bold "Takeaway:" sentence, and number exhibits x.y.
- Keep an assumption register with type (case fact / assumption / policy), source, ± profit impact and confidence.
- Print the formula for every bridge line; reconcile rounding differences openly.
- Count only the increment vs a moving status quo; build the discount rate from the bond yield plus a premium and show NPV across a ±2pp range.
- Steelman the stakeholder's preferred alternative with numbers, then show where it breaks (Year 3, terminal value).
- Use a governance table (Decision / Proposes / Approves / Rule) and a KPI table with "Scale if / Stop if" columns.
- Add illustrative UI mockups (checkout, studio, executive monitor) labelled "illustrative".
