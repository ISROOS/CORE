## Storyline pattern
- **Hook:** a standalone stat slide before the cover, then the persona Sophia (21, Bandung) introduced at slide 8, used in the journey map and **closed at slide 20 ("Meet Sophia again… But now")**. This is a book-ended persona arc.
- **Chain:** market tailwind → Gen Z behaviour → issue chain → survey proof (unaware/untrusted) → STP and sizing → competition → TOWS → journey gaps → ACT programs mapped to the funnel → roadmap → finance and risk → persona resolution.
- **Solution naming:** acronym **"ACT with TAVI"** = Attracting curiosity-driven awareness / Convert awareness into meaningful action / Transforming purchase into lasting loyalty. It maps directly onto the funnel (Awareness → Purchase → Loyalty) and onto the 3 problems. Programs get catchy names (#SplashYourLook, ReTAVI, TAVI Mini, Pop-Up Parade, Wave with TAVI, Prosperity for Gen Z). There is also a hashtag sign-off.

## Frameworks & analyses
Issue tree, problem priority, primary survey, STP, TAM/SAM/SOM (by people), competitor table plus perceptual map, SWOT/TOWS, persona, customer decision journey, current/future-state KPI targets, funnel-mapped campaign timeline, Hook/Proof/Offer conversion, gamification design, quarterly roadmap, cash flow / NPV / ROI, risk-mitigation table with impact×severity matrix.

## Numbers
- **Market and behaviour:** beauty US$9.1B, skincare ~35%, CAGR 4.6% to 2028. 63% of Gen Z willing to pay for ethically made products; 64% prefer durable quality; 87%/76% on community/values (cited, footnoted).
- **Survey:** n=95, with breakdowns as percentage bars (57% unaware; 41% unsure of quality; 62% would try at competitive prices; 79% of users cite skin suitability).
- **Sizing:** 68M Gen Z → 10M urban Gen Z in 5 cities → 500K customers (5% SAM in 1–3 yrs). This is a people count with no rupiah value.
- **KPI targets:** follower targets per channel; Pop-Up awareness uplift +12%, +1,000 visitors per event, +25,000 UGC impressions; KOL engagement rates (16.37%, 6.56%, 2.11%).
- **Financials:** 1-year quarterly model.
  - Inflow = "monthly marketing budget" Rp1.5B per quarter + trial-kit sales (Rp0.5B Q3, Rp0.75B Q4), totalling Rp7.25B.
  - Outflow Rp5.40B by pillar. NCF Rp1.85B, ROI 34.26% (1.85/5.40, which checks out), NPV Rp1.11B.
  - **Methodological flaw:** the company's own marketing budget is counted as *inflow*, so most of the "profit" is unspent budget. Real revenue is only Rp1.25B of kit sales. There is no discount rate, CAC, LTV or incremental-sales logic.

## Visual design system
- **Palette:** deep indigo/violet #250F75 (primary, title bars, nav), #4F3985, periwinkle #B8C2E9 / #7288C4, lavender #E1D4E7 / #AD95C1, ice #E6EBF4, lime-yellow highlight (as in the "Problem Priority" strip and TOWS chips). This follows TAVI's violet brand.
- **Fonts:** Poppins (body/titles), Montserrat (display), Canva Sans. Letter-spaced section labels ("A C T w i t h T A V I").
- **Layout grid:**
  - Top-left: a small ALL-CAPS italic section tag ("PROBLEM IDENTIFICATION") over a **two-line full-sentence action title**.
  - Top-right: a white logo tab (GMBCC · ParagonCorp · tavi).
  - Bottom: a **section tracker** with the current section bold and underlined, and a page-number chip.
- **Components:** violet header bars on cards; arrow chevrons between columns; 3D icons/emoji; Memoji persona avatar; journey line with red ✗ / green ✓ nodes; social-metric tiles with platform logos; concentric TAM circles; perceptual 2×2; swimlane Gantt; phone/website mockups and generated product photos; KOL screenshot cards; QR codes.
- **Density:** high (consulting style). Most slides have 3–4 blocks, but hierarchy and colour coding keep them readable.

## Why it likely won
- **Problem breakdown + data:** an issue chain with root causes, validated by its own n=95 survey (57% unaware), plus current→future KPI gaps. This hits the highest-weight rubric items.
- **A persona arc** (Sophia) gives emotional coherence and makes the impact concrete. The judge remembers "Sophia before vs after".
- **A funnel-mapped acronym:** each letter equals one funnel stage, one problem and 2–3 programs, so the logic reads instantly.
- **Execution realism for marketing:** named KOLs with engagement rates, mockups, a QR voucher mechanic, a quarterly roadmap and per-pillar risks.
- **Professional, consistent design** with action titles and a section tracker, close to a consulting deck.

## Weaknesses
- The financial model treats the marketing budget as revenue. The ROI and NPV are therefore not economically meaningful. There is no CAC, conversion→sales or LTV, and no sensitivity analysis.
- TAM/SAM/SOM is in people only, with no value. Its link to the 500K target in the financials is missing.
- There are too many programs (7) for a 1-year, Rp5.4B plan, and prioritisation is weakly argued beyond the "HIGH PRIORITY" tags.
- Generated or stock imagery (model photos, KOL quotes) may read as fabricated testimonials. They are not labelled "illustrative".
- Typos: "Rodmap", "greenable", "Semicondutcor"-style slips in places; "Over/Over 60%" duplicated text layers.

## Reusable techniques
- Use a **pre-cover hook slide**: one huge cited statistic on a brand-tinted photo, before the title slide.
- **Book-end the persona:** introduce them before the solution with a journey showing ✗ at each funnel break, then return at the conclusion with ✓ and "But now…".
- **Map acronym letters to funnel stages** (Awareness / Action / Loyalty) for marketing cases, so programs slot into a campaign-overview timeline.
- Use **current → gap → future** KPI tiles per channel (with platform logos) as the problem-to-target bridge.
- Design each program slide on the same layout: mockup → "How it helps" (3 cited percentages) → estimated impact/goals.
- Use the **Hook / Proof / Offer** frame for a conversion campaign (trial size, before/after content, then a QR voucher for full size).
- Give each slide an ALL-CAPS italic section tag plus a two-line action title, with a bottom section tracker.
- **Avoid** their mistake of booking the client's marketing budget as inflow. Model incremental revenue (reach × conversion × AOV × repeat) against cost, then derive ROI, CAC and LTV.
