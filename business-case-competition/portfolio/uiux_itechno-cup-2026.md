## Storyline / argument pattern
- **Problem → evidence → root cause → HMW → named solution → validated design → impact (SDGs)**. Same spine in both documents; the final compresses it and adds an emotional opener.
- **Hook:** final opens with a relatable 8-panel comic (emotional "pain journey" doubles as a storyboard/journey map) before any data. Prelim opens with a designed abstract that front-loads the outcome numbers (SUS 62.5→85.8).
- **Dual-sided problem framing:** "Both sides of the market want the same thing — certainty" (customers: what's broken/cost/when; artisans: work & payment). Strong unifying insight.
- **Each problem answered by a specific feature** ("solution mapping"): diagnosis gap→Scan, discovery→Instant Match, schedule/price→Agenda + estimate, materials/MSME→Pasar Material.
- **Branding:** name etymology (Tukang + Ku = "an artisan you can call your own"), Indonesian-word acronym **SIAP** ("ready": ready to diagnose/match/schedule/supply), tagline + hashtag, mascot character.
- **"However," pivot** on the research slide: pain stats then the 88.6% demand for the proposed concept — evidence of desirability before showing the solution.
- Bigger-picture lift: positions the app as an **instrument of economic formalisation** (verified income records → bankability/credit for informal workers and MSMEs), tied to SDG 8 & 9.

## How numbers are handled
- **Market context:** macro stats from BPS (70M+ households), ILO (3 in 5 informal workers), e-Conomy SEA (USD 90bn digital economy), APJII (79% internet). Final adds 56.9% urban and 57.1% homes 10+ years old.
- **Primary research numbers** drive the problem section: 78.9% artisans rely only on word of mouth, 64.9% customers rely on recommendations, 58.1% can't identify damage/trade, 73.0% paid more than quote, 55.4% didn't know material quantity, 84.2% artisans no income record, 88.6% want photo-based diagnosis.
- **UX metrics as hero numbers:** SUS 62.50→85.83 (benchmarked against 68 average / 80.3 top-decile), SEQ 5.12→6.38 (mean 5.75; Scan task 6.80), misclick 21.4%→9.5% (18/84 → 8/84), completion 8m42s→6m55s vs target range, 100% success. These appear on the abstract, SWOT (as a strength!) and testing slide — re-used as proof everywhere.
- **No market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM) or financial projections**; business model is qualitative (10% commission etc.).
- Citations APA-style in prelim; final deck has no on-slide sources.
- Caveat: SUS based on 6 testers, misclick on 3 testers — small samples presented confidently.

## Strengths (why it likely won)
- Complete, textbook UX process with **quantified validation** (two test rounds, SUS/SEQ/misclick improvements) — the strongest single proof element for a UI/UX jury.
- Every problem is mapped to a named feature; memorable Indonesian acronym (SIAP) and name story.
- Primary research (n=112, both sides of market) instead of only secondary data; "However, 88.6%…" concept-validation stat.
- Distinctive, highly consistent brand world (single red, mascot, toolbox, slogan) across both documents → instant recognisability.
- Polished hi-fi: realistic content, photos, states (issue detected, accuracy %, priority chips), consistent nav.
- Before/after iteration slide shows design reasoning, not just final screens.
- Strategic maturity beyond UI: three-sided marketplace, escrow to fight leakage, economic formalisation angle, risk/privacy considerations.
- Storytelling opener (comic) creates empathy in the first 10 seconds.

## Weaknesses / what a judge would criticize
- **Unfinished references in the prelim:** placeholders left in ("[lengkapi URL]", "[⚠ pastikan judul publikasi…]", "[⚠ cek judul & tahun laporan…]") — a credibility risk.
- **Figure caption mismatches** (prelim): interview quote cards captioned "User How-Might-We"; As-Is/To-Be table captioned "Affinity Map"; HMW text referenced but figure mislabelled. Nielsen cited as 2024 in text, 2020 in references.
- **Typos on slides:** "Backgroung Analysis", "Problem Statment", "servies", "Monsterrat", "Pallete", "Reguler/Semibold", "top decade" (for decile), hex codes "#16A46"/"#FDEE9E8" invalid; informal leftover heading "KEY INSIGHTNYA LOH YAA" on the testing slide; duplicated description text on Scan and Instant Match cards (slide 8); "Purchase Material" text says "through SIAP" instead of TukaKu.
- Design-system inconsistency between documents (primary #E63946 vs #ED1C24; navy appears only in final).
- Small usability samples (3–6 testers) presented as strong evidence; no statistical caveat.
- No market sizing, unit economics or rollout timeline; revenue model only listed.
- Final drops personas/empathy map — judges who didn't read the prelim lose the user context.
- SWOT/TOWS slide is text-heavy and hard to read in presentation.
- Uses ~9 typefaces in the deck (Canva drift) — minor, but not a disciplined type system.

## Reusable patterns
- **Open the final pitch with a comic/storyboard "day in the life"** (6–8 panels, captioned "1. A relaxing day at home… 8. Just another stressful day") — doubles as a journey map.
- **Designed abstract page** in a written proposal: headline, abstract, hero mockup, and a bottom KPI strip containing the outcome metrics (before→after) and SDG tiles.
- **Local-language acronym for features** that is itself a meaningful word (SIAP = "ready") + name etymology paragraph.
- **"Problem → feature" mapping sentence/table:** each researched problem gets exactly one named feature.
- **Research slide flow with arrows:** Respondents → Key findings → "However, X% want [concept]" → Main problems.
- **Quantified iteration loop:** two test phases, report SUS (vs 68 / 80.3 benchmarks), SEQ per task, misclick %, completion time vs target; then show before/after screens with the per-task score change.
- **Re-use hero metrics everywhere** (abstract, SWOT strengths, testing, conclusion).
- **Persistent section nav bar** at the slide bottom + kicker/title/subtitle header + logo pill = instant deck structure.
- **Bento design-system slide** (numbered tiles: brand, palette, type scale, inputs, nav, buttons).
- **Mascot character** as a recurring guide pointing at content; sticker-style 3D feature wordmarks.
- **As-Is vs To-Be table by phase** with icons (diagnosis, discovery, pricing, scheduling, materials, credibility, records).
- **One persona per market side** in a multi-sided platform + empathy map for the side most tied to impact.
- **TOWS with concrete moves** (MVP scope, district-by-district cold-start, "never price a low-confidence scan" fallback) shows product-risk thinking.
- **Canvas/tech spec table** (frame size, tool, screen count vs minimum, OS targets) — easy rubric points.
- **Appendix screen sequences**: 3 phones per feature with spaced-caps step labels.
- QA checklist to avoid repeating mistakes: remove placeholder notes, check captions vs figures, spell-check slide text, validate hex codes, remove internal jokes.
