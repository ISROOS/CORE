## Storyline pattern
- **Hook:** an aspirational, real-world photo cover of smiling ploggers, then a "Tentang Produk" page that names the solution and gives a demo video *before* the background. The answer comes first.
- **Problem → cause → solution → impact:** the issue is litter (26.6% litter in public, Goodstats). The opportunity is that plogging is growing (+35%) but awareness is low (9/10 don't know it). The user-level causes are not knowing where to drop sorted trash (96%/87%), distance to drop-offs (90%) and lack of motivation/reward. Solution: RECOVER features. Impact: SDG 3/11 and stakeholder outcomes.
- The conclusion uses a **"paradox" framing**: rising interest in health running vs. dirty running routes.
- **Solution naming:** product "Plogee" + English acronym **RECOVER** (Recap, Event, Community, Verification, Reward, Tracker… drop-Off) styled as a big italic display wordmark with satellite labels. The letters map to features, not to root causes.

## Numbers
- **Context:** 26.6% litter (Goodstats 2024); plogging up 35% in 2 yrs (rri.co.id 2024); 9/10 unaware; 36.3% exercise weekly; 288 kcal/30 min; 88.58M Gen Z + millennials (BPS 2025).
- **Primary research:** survey n=67 (60% aged 17–35). Run frequency 47% 2–3×/wk. Interest in plogging 63% very interested + 21% interested. Pain points 96% don't know how to sort, 87% don't know where to drop, 90% distance, 59% no time. Motivations: impact 96%, reward 90%, social 80%, health 84%.
- **Usability:**
  - Completion time: phase 1 9m16s, phase 2 7m41s (target 8m20s–10m12s).
  - Success rate: 100% in both phases.
  - Misclick: 25.7% (19/74 clicks, 3 testers) in phase 1, 12.2% (9/74) in phase 2.
  - SEQ: 4.89 → 6.5 (the caption says 6.44).
  - SUS: 59.17 → 86.67 (6 users; the caption says 80.33).
- **No market sizing, revenue model or financials.** The business side is only "partnerships / sponsorship" in the STP table.
- Sources: APA in-text, plus a reference list (journals, rri, detik, Goodstats).

## Why it likely won (typical UX rubric: research/problem, design process, UI quality, usability validation, feasibility/impact, presentation)
- **Complete, textbook DT coverage** where each rubric item is visible as its own titled sub-section (a judge can tick boxes).
- **Proof of improvement:** two test rounds with quantified gains (SUS 59→87, misclick 25.7→12.2%), plus a 3-step visual iteration.
- **Breadth of craft:** phone + **smartwatch** UI, AI route and trash scan, QR drop-off, demo video and Figma QR. It feels like a real product.
- **Prioritisation matrix** shows judgement, not just feature-listing.
- **Strong first impression:** a real-photo cover and the answer-first product page with the RECOVER wordmark.

## Weaknesses
- **Numbers disagree with themselves:** the SUS ring shows 86.67 but the caption says 80.33, and the SEQ ring shows 6.5 but the caption says 6.44. A label error also appears on the iteration page ("Halaman Social" used twice).
- **Tiny samples:** 3 testers for misclick, 6 for SUS, with no caveat.
- The RECOVER letters are forced and map to features rather than root causes. The acronym order on the wordmark doesn't follow the card order.
- No business model, costs or rollout. "Hasil yang diharapkan" is qualitative only.
- Typos ("Pegawai Swata", "prngguna", "Successibility Rate", "TWOS"). Garbled glyphs in the SWOT export.
- Survey statistics read as multi-select percentages without stating n per question.

## Differences vs Wijaya Team house style (ITECHNO 🥇 TukaKu)
**They do, Wijaya doesn't (or did less):**

- **Answer-first product page** (p3) with a demo-video QR before the background.
- **Impact × effort prioritisation 2×2** of features (ITECHNO jumped from HMW to SIAP).
- **Second device (smartwatch)** UI and an IoT angle.
- **Full-bleed hi-fi showcase page** with the prototype QR, and a **3-iteration** sequence per screen (Wijaya showed before/after pairs).
- **Rich picture** stakeholder illustration.
- **Testimonial quotes from testers** placed next to the SUS result.

**Wijaya does, they don't:**
- Issue chain → root cause → pillar mapping. SIAP letters each answer a problem, while RECOVER letters are just features.
- A recurring human hook (comic/persona carried through).
- SUS benchmarking vs 68/80.3.
- A strategic angle (economic formalisation), risk/privacy in the TOWS, and English-language polish.
- One mascot/brand world. Plogee has a brand, but no character.

**Same skeleton:** both follow the same BINUS-style UI/UX proposal template (DT 5 stages, As-Is/To-Be, HMW, solution mapping, acronym, Crazy 8 → wireframe → design system → hi-fi, heuristic → UT 1/2 → SEQ/SUS → iteration, spec table, nav map).

## Reusable techniques
- Put a **"Tentang Produk" answer-first page** right after the abstract: hero phone-in-hand photo, demo-video QR, acronym wordmark + one card per feature.
- Add an **impact × effort matrix** between HMW and the main solution to justify the MVP scope.
- Use a **KPI strip per test phase** (avg completion time vs target range · success rate · misclick with raw counts x/y), then **ring gauges with before → after arrows** for SEQ/SUS.
- Put **2 tester testimonial bubbles** under the SUS result as qualitative proof.
- Show an **iteration triplet** (Iterasi 1 → 2 → 3) per key screen instead of a single before/after.
- Close the hi-fi section with a **full-bleed brand-colour showcase page** (all screens + Figma QR).
- Use a **real-photo cover** of the target activity (not illustration) for instant context.
- Add a **companion-device UI** (watch/widget) to show ecosystem thinking.
- QA lesson: make captions match the charts. The 80.33 vs 86.67 mismatch is exactly the error class in Wijaya's recurring-mistakes list.
