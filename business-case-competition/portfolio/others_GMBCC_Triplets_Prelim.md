## Storyline pattern
- **Hook:** a company-strength opening (Paragon as halal-beauty leader). The tension is that consumers want premium packaging while circularity is needed. SCQA sits in Appendix 1.
- **Chain:** KQ → issue tree → root causes → frameworks narrow the focus to Wardah (about 70% of revenue, so most of the waste) and to the *use phase* (about 70% of packaging not recovered, via LCT) → 3 pillars → timeline, risks, finance → before/after impact.
- **Solution naming:** an **acronym of pillars, "CARE with Paragon"** (Continuous sustainable refillable / Accessible circular collection / Reinforce education & engagement), with named programs under each (CareCycle app, ParaGreen Week, Eco Challenge). This is the same device as Wijaya's acronyms.

## Frameworks & analyses
SCQA, issue tree, primary survey, STP, SWOT→TOWS, **Life Cycle Thinking**, PESTLE, solution matrix, timeline, Gantt, risk register with impact×severity matrix, cash flow / NPV / ROI, before/after impact, take-back process flowchart, app UI prototype (Figma).

## Numbers
- **Survey:** n=53, 81.1% Gen Z. 41.5% like refillable makeup, 54.7% like attractive design, 67.9% hesitant to pay more for eco packaging.
- **Cited facts:**
  - Indonesia recycles only 9% of 3.2Mt of plastic waste.
  - Only 7–9% of plastic is recycled, rising to as much as 40% participation with integrated digital systems (WEF).
  - Refill cuts carbon by up to 70% (Ellen MacArthur).
  - 78% of Gen Z interested in zero waste vs 29% who understand their impact.
- **Pillar C impact:** per refill, −34% plastic, −37% CO₂, −40% fossil energy, −34% water. Pods priced 25% below full size. 850 kg of plastic saved per 50k bundles.
- **Financials:** ROI 44.62%, NPV Rp70B, NCF Rp77.2B, cash-flow growth 107.84% over a 10-year horizon to 2035. The derivation is only in the appendix cash-flow image; no assumptions or discount rate appear in the body.

## Why it likely won (advanced)
- **A clear problem breakdown:** issue tree to root cause, then deliberate narrowing (Wardah, then the use phase) using data. Rubrics typically put the most weight here.
- **A memorable acronym** whose 3 pillars map to 3 issue branches, with a solution matrix showing "Why?" backed by a cited statistic.
- **Tangible solution visuals:** refill pod mockups, the CareCycle app with a Figma link, a vending machine. Each pillar has quantified environmental impact.
- **A complete implementation package** (phased timeline, risk matrix, NPV/ROI) plus the full set of frameworks in the appendix. It reads as "thorough" without crowding the body.

## Weaknesses
- The finance is thin in the body: only headline bubbles, with no assumptions, revenue logic or discount rate. "Cash growth 107.84%" is vague.
- The survey (n=53) is reported without caveats, and its findings (67.9% won't pay more) are not used to set the price.
- Frameworks are mostly summarised in prose (PESTLE paragraph). They are somewhat checklist-like.
- Small typos: "strategis", "Souce", "Wardah will added", "In 995". The pillar name differs between places ("Refillable" vs "Reusable").
- The impact numbers (−34% plastic, etc.) are not clearly sourced to the specific pod design.

## Differences vs Wijaya Team house style
| Triplets does, Wijaya doesn't (or less) | Wijaya does, Triplets doesn't |
|---|---|
| **Poster-per-pillar pages:** a single full-page Canva infographic holding the rationale, mockups and an impact-stat column | Formula, source and assumption tag on every financial figure; sensitivity and downside cases |
| **Life Cycle Thinking** to locate the hotspot (use phase) | Per-pillar KPI and budget lines |
| Explicit narrowing logic (largest brand → largest waste → focus) | Pilot and stage gates with capital at risk; explicit ask |
| A **solution matrix** with a "Why?" row backed by external statistics | Human persona hook (Triplets adds one only in the SF) |

| Formal academic prose wrapped around designed figures | Full-sentence action titles |
- Both teams use acronym pillars, an issue tree and appendix-heavy frameworks. On structure, Triplets is close to the Wijaya house style.

## Reusable techniques
- Add a **narrowing funnel**: portfolio → biggest brand by revenue share → biggest waste stage (LCT) → focus. It justifies scope in two sentences.
- Use a **solution summary matrix** (pillars as columns; Objective / What / Problem / Details / Why as rows), with a cited statistic in every "Why" cell.
- Give each pillar a **full-page poster** with a header pill, a dashed-box rationale, a product mockup and an "Impact in every X" stat column of 4 icons and big percentages.
- Use **impact×severity 3×3 grids** with numbered circles keyed to a risk→mitigation flow.
- Show **before/after impact** as a mirrored diagram scored on the same 5 dimensions.
- In a paper format, keep the frameworks to one paragraph each and push the full canvases to the appendix with hyperlinks ("Appendix 5").
