## Storyline / argument pattern
- **Hook:** young adults *still use Pepsodent* but trade down to Basic. Each switch drops the margin from 55% to 38%, and deep promos (−44%) make Premium less profitable per unit than Basic.
- **Problem → root cause:** 4 linked symptoms in a vicious cycle. RC1 (benefit with no personal measure, 44%), RC2 (expensive entry, only full tube or deep discount, 39%), RC3 (no variant guidance on TikTok/IG, 24%) and RC4 (unsupported routine, slow results) are ordered by the share of switching reasons.
- **Solution naming:** **TRUE** = Tailor the Variant / Reduce the Entry Cost / Unveil the Results / Embed the Habit. "TRUE" echoes the theme "Dari Klaim ke Bukti" (from claim to proof): Premium's benefit becomes personally *proven* (true).
- **Signature rule:** the **"Aturan Harga" (Price Rule)**: price per 100 g never drops below the refill price (Rp37,900). The Starter Kit at Rp15,900/40 g lowers the trial cost by 62% without cutting the per-gram price. This is the same guardrail device as IBSC's "50% Profit Rule".
- **Mechanism:** TikTok/IG → 60-second WhatsApp quiz (Tanya Dokter Gigi, with red-flag triage to a dentist) → Starter Kit → 14-day personal results note → 66-day habit programme plus refill subscription.
- **Impact (honest about negatives):** operating profit is positive from 2030 (Rp7.7bn in 2031). **The 5-year NPV is −Rp3.5bn**, and +Rp6.8bn with terminal value. Losses are capped at Rp5.8bn if G1 fails. The cost of doing nothing is about Rp326bn of gross profit lost over 5 years if share keeps falling 1 pt/yr. TRUE recovers only 0.7–1.0 of the 7 lost points, positioned as "phase one".
- **Close:** pilot in Bandung–Surabaya, expanded only if G1 and G2 pass. Growth "through proof, not discounts".

## How numbers are handled
- **All consumer insights come from the case survey.** The team states plainly that no primary research was done yet and schedules four studies before launch (L15), with thresholds that would change the strategy.
- **Calculation register (L19):** each headline number is listed with its formula or source (e.g., price gap 3.3× = Rp41,900 ÷ Rp12,700; kit Rp39,750/100 g = 15,900 ÷ 40 × 100; share change formula).
- **Assumptions are benchmarked where possible** (kit→tube funnel 19% vs 35% for product sampling, Retail Customer Experience 2008) and otherwise made into gates (completion ≥40% = G1; 6-month retention ≥78% = G2).
- **Financials show uncomfortable truths:** negative 5-year NPV, payback beyond 5 years, IRR −3.5% in the base case, and LTV/CAC of only 1.2×. These are balanced by terminal value and the cost of inaction.
- Cannibalization (15%) and displaced Basic gross profit are netted out in the bridge, and halo effects are excluded from all scenarios.
- Downside is computed "without gates"; with gates the programme stops at G1 with a Rp5.8bn loss.

## Strengths
- A coherent theme across every layer: "Dari Klaim ke Bukti" → TRUE acronym → Proof Engine → per-variant measurable "Bukti" → claims only from a clinical trial.
- Root causes are prioritized by data (switching-reason shares), not asserted.
- Exceptional intellectual honesty: negative NPV, LTV/CAC 1.2×, the absence of primary research acknowledged, and only 0.7–1.0 of 7 points recovered. Each is paired with gates, research and a cost-of-inaction framing.
- Ethics and regulation are treated as design (dentist triage, no reward for results, BPOM, UU PDP, conflict-of-interest disclosure). That is credible for a health brand.
- Traceability: the calculation register plus "Rincian › Lampiran X" link chips from every pillar.
- Unusually rigorous validation design (A/B pilot, fake-door test, Gabor-Granger, an RCT with a power calculation).

## Weaknesses / what a judge would criticize
- **The headline economics look unattractive:** 5-year NPV −Rp3.5bn, IRR −3.5%, payback beyond 5 years, and a +0.7–1.0 pt share recovery out of 7 lost. A judge may conclude the solution does not solve the problem at the required scale, which is likely a key reason for not advancing (if it did not).
- Over-engineered for a preliminary round (clinical trial power calcs, ICC/design effect), while the core marketing idea (quiz + sample kit + WhatsApp coaching) may seem incremental rather than bold.
- WhatsApp coaching over 14–66 days assumes high engagement (45% completion) with no public benchmark, as the team concedes.
- Body text in Times New Roman beside Poppins UI feels academic and inconsistent with the vivid cover.
- Very dense pages; English pillar names inside an Indonesian document mix languages.

## Reusable patterns
- Make the theme a before→after phrase ("Dari Klaim ke Bukti") and make the acronym (TRUE) semantically reinforce it.
- Order root causes by the data share behind each (survey %), and show rejected hypotheses in a small table.
- Add a named price or financial guardrail rule shown as a pill at the centre of the solution cycle diagram.
- Use a solution cycle diagram: 4 pillar blocks around a central "engine" card, with arrow labels stating the conversion between steps (e.g., "42% buy the tube").
- Use KPI chip rows per pillar ending in a "Details › Appendix X" link chip for traceability.
- Keep a calculation register appendix: every headline number → formula/source.
- Include a cost-of-inaction table to justify spend when the NPV is weak.
- Gate on behavioural metrics with numeric thresholds (G1 completion ≥40%, G2 retention ≥78%), and state the maximum loss if G1 fails.
- Include a primary research plan with "threshold that would change the strategy" when only case data exists.
- Write persona cards built from case data, labelled as archetypes rather than interviews.
- Show a "Dampak bagi Tiga Pihak" (impact on three parties) strip: consumer / company / society.
- **Lesson:** when the honest NPV is negative, reframe the scope (phase one) *and* lead with an upside path; otherwise judges read it as "doesn't pay".
