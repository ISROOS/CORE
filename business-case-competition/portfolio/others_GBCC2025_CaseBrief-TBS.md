## The question (semifinal)
- **Objective:** ARAH reaches an **EBITDA of US$1.5M in 2025** and **US$2.5M in 2026**.
- **KQ1:** How can ARAH reach its 2025 EBITDA target?
- **KQ2:** How can TBS use its waste-management reputation to lead the brown→green industrial transformation as first mover in low-carbon?
- **Required deck content:** market and competitive analysis; growth strategy (segments, geography, partnerships, differentiation); feasibility (profitability forecast, **ROI, IRR**, timeline); innovation and acceleration (new products/services).

## Scoring rubric (semifinal)
| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Problem breakdown | **25%** |
| Solution | **30%** |
| Usage of data | **20%** |
| Storyline | 15% |
| Design | 5% |
| Business English writing | 5% |
- **Design is worth only 5%.** 75% goes to problem breakdown, solution and data. Contrast this with campus BPCs, where polish carries more weight.

## What the founder letter says judges reward (paraphrased)
1. **Iterative thinking:** your "best idea" has probably already been considered and rejected by the company. Go deeper and explain why it would work now.
2. **Expert interviews, not just Google:** contact practitioners on LinkedIn; one hour with an expert beats hours of desk research.
3. **Quantitative rigor:** the best ideas are the easiest to execute, have the highest impact (monetary and non-monetary) and the lowest risk. Quantify everything. Judges can "see through" random primary surveys and inflated markups.
4. **Use AI but don't rely on it.** A suggested prompt is to role-play the owner/SVP challenging practicality, risk and reward.
5. **A compelling exec summary:** an intriguing hook in the deck title, then a high-impact one-page summary, because judges skim hundreds of entries.
6. Motto: "Think like a consultant, innovate like a scientist, execute like a startup."

## Why this matters / differences vs Wijaya Team house style
- **Matches Wijaya:** a one-page exec summary, a number on everything, and feasibility and risk. Wijaya's formula/assumption tags and stage gates answer "quantitative rigor" directly.
- **Gaps for Wijaya:**
  - **Expert interviews** are explicitly rewarded and Wijaya rarely cites them. Add an "Expert voices" box with name, role and a quote.
  - **A hard financial target** (EBITDA bridge from today to US$1.5M) calls for a **waterfall/bridge chart** rather than a pillar list.
  - **IRR** is explicitly required, alongside ROI and NPV.
  - **The 10-page cap** means an acronym with more than 4 pillars won't fit. Keep 3 pillars and move the detail to the appendix.
  - The warning against **primary surveys** cuts against the small-n surveys Wijaya sometimes uses. Prefer company and market data plus experts.
- **Brand cue:** the steel blue + teal + Nunito look of TBS2030.

## Reusable techniques
- Before designing, convert the rubric into a page budget (for example: 2–3 pages on problem breakdown, 3–4 on solution, a data source on every page).
- When the case states a numeric target, open with a **target-gap bridge** (current EBITDA → gap → contribution of each initiative → target).
- Rate every initiative on **ease × impact × risk**, the founder's three non-negotiables, in a matrix.
- Add an "Interviewed experts" strip with names, roles and dates, plus a one-line insight from each.
- Put the hook in the deck title and close with a one-pager the judge can read in 30 seconds.
