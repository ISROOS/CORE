# [SemiFinal Top 7] BPC – Genexpo x Bank Indonesia 2026 — "JELANTRA / JELANTARA"

## Competition & type
- **Type:** Business Plan Competition (GenExpo 2026, with Bank Indonesia). Team "Bravie": Dicky V. C, Nicky Gilbert, Michaella. Dated "Tanggerang, 30 April 2026".
- **Result:** **Semifinal, Top 7** (did not reach the podium).
- **Idea:** The same used-cooking-oil smart collection machine later reworked into the 🥉 UBM entry. Here the user payout goes to an in-app balance that can be **cashed out to an e-wallet or spent via QRIS**. This is an explicit nod to the Bank Indonesia sponsor; BI's QRIS document is the first reference.

## Document type & format
- `Proposal.pdf`: **28 pages**, portrait US Letter (612×792 pt), 22.6 MB (heavy embedded images).
- Body in Times New Roman (live text). Figures are pasted images with sans fonts.
- Page decoration: palm-leaf corners at the top and palm-fruit corners at the bottom on every page (a palm-oil motif).

## Storyline / argument pattern
- **Order:** background statistics → environmental harm → economic value of UCO → conventional collectors are inaccessible → JELANTRA machine.
- **Solution framing:** immediate cash ("Tukar Minyak Jadi Uang"); convenience (24/7 machines at minimarkets); a digital wallet / QRIS link (sponsor alignment).
- **Positioning logic:** a perceptual map with axes *ease of access × economic value to the user*. Direct dumping = high access / low value; collectors = low access / medium value; JELANTRA = high / high.
- **Naming:** the brand is inconsistent. The text says "JELANTRA" while the logo and figure captions say "JELANTARA". The marketing acrostic is K.E.Y (Indonesian words).

## Frameworks & analyses used
Vision/Mission · service flow · STP (geographic / demographic / psychographic / behavioural) · targeting criteria · perceptual map · competitor matrix · TAM/SAM/SOM · K.E.Y marketing funnel · distribution plan · revenue model (4 streams) · cost projection · revenue projection · ROI / PBP / NPV · sources & uses of funds · 12-month timeline · risk matrix (likelihood × impact, described in text) with mitigation tables split into **business side vs customer side** · SWOT and BMC in the appendix.

## How numbers are handled
- **Market sizing** is identical to the UBM version:
  - TAM 2–3 bn L = Rp16–24 T
  - SAM 600–900 M L = Rp4.8–7.2 T
  - SOM 3–4.5 M L = Rp12–18 bn
- **Unit economics:** buy from users at Rp4,000/L, sell to industry at Rp8,000/L (a 50% margin).
- **Costs:** a single "Estimasi Biaya Awal" table that is really **total annual costs**. Rp790 M (Y1) → 1.83 bn (Y5) across 12 lines (legal, R&D, server, ads, salaries, influencers, new machines, distribution, rent, maintenance, logistics, reserve).
- **Revenue:** Rp1.45 bn → 2.36 → 3.84 → 5.81 → **8.28 bn** (Y5), across 6 streams: household/UMKM oil, restaurant oil, sponsorship/branding, data/ESG, CSR campaigns, other.
- **KPIs claimed:** ROI 40%, PBP 3.5 yrs, NPV Rp407.64 M (10%).
- **Funding:** Rp790 M = founders 24% + angel 38% + grants/competitions 19% + KUR loan 13% + location partners 6%.
- **Consistency problems (judge-visible):**
  1. Oil purchase cost (Rp4,000/L ≈ 50% of oil revenue) **does not appear in the cost table**, so profit is overstated.
  2. Revenue minus costs gives a **+Rp660 M profit in Y1** and Σ5y ≈ Rp15.2 bn. That cannot produce a 3.5-year payback, a 40% ROI or an NPV of only Rp408 M. The KPIs don't reconcile with the tables.
  3. A chart titled "Proyeksi **Akumulasi Laba Bersih**" actually plots **annual revenue** (legend: "Total Pendapatan").
  4. A leftover sentence says the model "lebih masuk akal untuk kebutuhan lomba bisnis plan". This reads like an unedited AI/draft note.
  5. Most references have no URL or specific title and look generic. Partner claims (Apical) are not evidenced.

## Strengths
- A **sponsor-tailored feature** (QRIS / e-wallet payout, citing Bank Indonesia), which is good alignment with a BI-backed competition.
- The infographic exec summary on one page covers model, market, finance and impact.
- Concrete distribution logic using real minimarket outlet counts.
- **Two-sided risk register** (business vs user) with icons and specific mitigations.
- A sources-and-uses table that includes grants and KUR, which shows financing awareness.
- Formal proposal structure (foreword, ToC, chapters, references, appendix) that matches typical Indonesian BPC guidelines.

## Weaknesses / what a judge would criticise
- **Financial model is broken:** missing COGS, KPIs inconsistent with the tables, a mislabelled chart.
- **Brand inconsistency:** JELANTRA vs JELANTARA, plus the "EXCECUTIVE" typo.
- The draft-like sentence about "kebutuhan lomba" is a credibility hit.
- References are generic, with no data tables linking claims to sources. "Berbagai studi" is used without a citation.
- The risk heatmap is described but not actually shown (only tables).
- Clashing neon-green Excel tables against a refined infographic style; the palm decoration crowds the margins; the 22 MB file size is a submission-portal risk.
- No validation (survey, pilot, LOI). The machine count behind the revenue is never stated.

## Reusable patterns
- Add **one feature that maps to the sponsor's agenda** (BI → QRIS / digital payments) and cite the sponsor's own publication.
- Write the distribution plan around **real network counts** (e.g. outlet numbers of retail chains) to make the rollout tangible.
- Structure risks as a **risk table split by stakeholder side** (business vs customer) with icons and one-line mitigations.
- Include a **sources-and-uses / funding mix table** (founder, angel, grant, KUR, partner in-kind) with percentages.
- Use a **local-language acrostic funnel** (K.E.Y = Kenalkan–Ekspos–Yakinkan) with a short campaign line ("Tukar Minyak Jadi Uang").
- Thematic page frame tied to the industry (here palm leaves / fruit), but keep it subtle.
- **Lesson (versus the 🥉 UBM version):** the upgrade that won a podium was splitting COGS from opex, adding an itemised capex, making KPIs reconcile, adding high-margin add-on streams (ESG SaaS, carbon credit), and adding a hardware-level operations chapter.
