# B-Startion 2024 (BINUS B-Preneur): Tim Ascendio, "Watt's Up"

## Competition / type / round / placement
- **Competition:** B-Startion 2024, run by the BINUS Entrepreneur (B-Preneur) student club in Tangerang. The originality statement is dated 23 Oct 2024.
- **Type:** business plan / startup proposal for an IoT smart-meter app. The brief seems to be a sustainable smart-city app; the entry has an SDG section and a "Strategi Keberlanjutan" chapter.
- **Round:** semifinal submission (file name "BabakSemifinal"). Team: Clairine Sabatini Nayoan, Elbert, Shane Alan Hartono (all BINUS).
- **Placement:** winner per the user; the actual placement is unknown.

## Format
- 25 pages, A4 portrait, written in Indonesian in academic thesis style (BAB I–IV, Daftar Isi, Referensi, Lampiran, Turnitin page with 4% similarity).
- Typography is Times New Roman 12 for the body and Arial in the figures, i.e. a Word document template. Every page has a branded header and footer.

## Storyline pattern
- **Hook:** a macro statistic (households are the 2nd-largest energy user at 30.7%) plus foreign evidence (UK waste, a 10–15% saving in China). There is no persona, no local primary data and no story.
- **Problem → root cause → solution → impact:** the problem is energy waste caused by low awareness, and the solution is a smart meter plus app. There is no explicit root-cause analysis or issue tree. Impact is shown only as financial profit and an SDG mapping, with no kWh or CO₂ savings figure.
- **Solution naming:** a pun brand name ("Watt's Up") with a lightning-hexagon logo. It is not an acronym and has no pillars; the features are listed one by one.

## Frameworks & analyses
STP, 4P with actual vs augmented product, TAM/SAM/SOM, cost budget, revenue analysis, 4-year cumulative profit, ROI, payback period, Gantt chart, risk heatmap with mitigation tables, SDG mapping, BMC (appendix), system architecture (appendix). Frameworks appear as textbook templates with little "so what".

## Numbers
- **Market:** TAM Rp1.356T = 1,686,385 Tangerang households (BPS Banten) × Rp804k/yr. SAM is set at 10% and SOM at 1% of SAM (Rp1.36B). The Rp804k/yr figure is never derived.
- **Pricing:** Basic Rp59k, Premium Rp79k, top-up admin fee Rp2,750, installation Rp175k. The text then contradicts itself, saying a 20% developer discount gives "Rp120k / Rp239k per unit".
- **Costs:** about Rp2.9B in year 1, mostly salaries (Rp780M) and smart-meter hardware (1,500 × Rp800k = Rp1.2B).
- **Revenue:** Rp1.47B in year 1, from 900 Basic + 600 Premium subscriptions plus 1,500 installations. Revenue reaches Rp5.96B by year 4.
- **Returns:** ROI 31.79% over 4 years (Rp14.55B revenue vs Rp11.02B cost); payback 2 years 10 months.
- **Consistency problems:**
  - Year-2 cumulative profit is −Rp1.778B in the table but −Rp1.178B in the chart; the chart value is correct.
  - "Tahun Pertama: Rp2,904,500" is missing a "000".
  - Year-1 revenue is Rp1.4685B in Table 5 but Rp1.4706B in the profit table.
  - The ROI uses revenue as the "gain", which is the same error as on the Wijaya mistakes list.
- **Research:** no primary research.

## Visual design system
- **Palette:** royal blue #155EE7, navy #1A3C8C, sun yellow #FADF5F (the B-Startion brand yellow), light grey #E6E6E6, slate #797F95.
- **Fonts:** Times New Roman for the body, Arial in tables. Canva-made figures use rounded sans fonts.
- **Layout:** a single text column. The header has the app logo pill on the left and the B-Startion pill on the right; the footer has a blue diagonal geometric band.
- **Components:** bordered Word tables, blue-header tables, a 3-circle TAM/SAM/SOM Venn, gradient heatmaps, a Gantt chart with a coloured header, phone mockups on a light background.
- **Density:** medium. Text-heavy pages alternate with full-page figures. The cover is the strongest designed page.

## Why it likely won
- **Completeness:** it ticks every typical BPC rubric item (market, marketing mix, finance, timeline, risk, SDG, BMC, prototype screens) with the expected framework at each step.
- **The app feels tangible:** a full design system page and two pages of mockups plus a system architecture. Judges at a B-Preneur event reward a "buildable product".
- **Local data:** TAM is based on official BPS Banten household counts and the target market is a specific city (Tangerang, home of the BINUS Alam Sutera campus).
- **A positive story:** ROI, payback and a 4-year cumulative profit chart turning positive. At semifinal level the level of polish beat the rigor of the numbers.

## Weaknesses
- There is no problem analysis (no issue tree, root cause or primary data) and no persona.
- The numbers have internal errors (see above). The ROI is computed on revenue, the pricing contradicts itself and the hardware subsidy logic is not explained.
- Frameworks are dumped without insight (STP and 4P are generic).
- There is no competitor analysis, although many smart-plug and PLN Mobile apps exist.
- Impact on energy and CO₂ is never quantified despite the sustainability theme.
- Times New Roman thesis styling looks dated next to the designed figures.

## Differences vs Wijaya Team house style
| Ascendio does, Wijaya doesn't | Wijaya does, Ascendio doesn't |
|---|---|
| Academic BAB format with a signed originality letter and a Turnitin page bundled in | Acronym pillars, each mapped to a root cause, a KPI and a budget line |
| A dedicated **app design-system page** (logo, type, icons, colour chips) | An issue chain (SMART question → problems → root causes) |
| Separate risk heatmaps for the company side and the customer side | Human-hook persona carried through the document |
| BMC and system architecture as appendix items | A formula, source and assumption tag on every number; downside/base/upside cases and sensitivity |
| A simple ROI formula shown inline | Pilot, stage gates and capital at risk; a "what we will NOT do" slide |
| | Full-sentence action titles and a one-page infographic exec summary |
- The takeaway: a less rigorous entry can still win a campus BPC on completeness and product polish. Wijaya's rigor is an advantage only if the product feels as tangible as Ascendio's mockups.

## Reusable techniques
- Add a **product design-system page** (logo, palette, type, icon set, key screens) when the solution is an app. It shows the product can actually be built.
- Show **dual-perspective risk heatmaps** (company vs customer) with numbered dots that link to a mitigation table.
- Put **TAM on official household counts** (BPS by kabupaten/kota) for one launch city.
- Put a **cumulative-profit bar chart** that crosses zero next to the payback statement. It makes break-even visible at a glance.
- Put the competition brand pill in the header on every page. It costs nothing and looks "native" to the organiser.
- Use as a QA warning: errors between table and chart (−1.778B vs −1.178B) are exactly the kind a sharp judge spots. Cross-check every figure.
