# IBCC BRAND UI 2026: The Incredibles, "OTO T.A.P: Save first. Own Next." (Summit Oto Finance)

Source: `Proposal Lomba/IBCC BRAND UI 2026_The Incredibles.pdf` (66 MB). Renders are in `renders/ibcc/inc_*`.

## Competition / type / round / placement
- **Competition:** International Business Case Competition (IBCC), BRAND UI 2026. The case owner is PT Summit Oto Finance (SOF; SMBC Indonesia group, motorcycle/car multifinance, merged with Oto Multiartha in Oct 2026). The theme is "sustainable transformation". The key question, quoted from the case, is: develop an innovative financial product for the next generation that complies with regulation, drives sustainable growth and mitigates risk.
- **Type:** although the competition is labelled "BRAND", this case is a **financial-product / business-model case with a brand component** (only 32.9% of Gen Z know SOF). It is not a pure IMC case. In other words, **IBCC BRAND UI is a general business case competition**, and the case type changes each year.
- **Round:** written proposal round. A Turnitin report is attached (2% similarity), which implies an originality check is required.
- **Placement:** winner per user; the placement is not stated in the document.
- **Team:** "The Incredibles", 3 people (Adrian Aulia, Natalia Jesslyn, Timothy Othniel).

## Format
- 52 pages, A4 portrait.

## Storyline pattern
- **Hook:** the company's pain (new financing +2.52%, 2025 net loss Rp505B) set against the market shift (paylater +54.65% YoY) and the Gen Z aspiration of a motorcycle as their first productive asset. The **persona "Rani"** runs through it: she appears on the cover mockup and in the plafon worked example (income Rp4.5m → plafon Rp15.5m at Rp814k/month = 18% of income).
- **Problem → root cause:** 3 challenges:
  1. **Credit-readiness gap:** young people discover SLIK damage only when rejected; 19–34s hold 48.22% of bad P2P loans.
  2. **Savings-to-ownership barrier:** saving is never counted as creditworthiness.
  3. **Access and growth constraints:** a dealer-led, paper-heavy process, multi-fee pricing of ~39% effective, and no deposits allowed.
- **Solution:** **OTO T.A.P = Cari Tahu Dulu → Ayo Nabung → Pakai Langsung** ("check → save → own"). It is an Indonesian-language, sequential customer journey run as a Jenius mini-app. Jenius holds the money, while SOF owns the customer, the brand and the credit. The design is regulation-aware: SOF cannot take deposits, so savings sit in the group bank.
- **Impact:** investment Rp3.7B, payback ~1.1 years, NPV Rp171B at 12%, IRR 82.3%, 220k contracts 2027–31. The positioning shift is "dealer-based lender" → "first-asset partner".
- **Solution naming:** a product brand (OTO = SOF's sub-brand), the acronym T.A.P drawn from the key word of each step, and a tagline ("Save first. Own Next."). Feature names are colloquial Indonesian: Kelas OTO, Nabung Receh, Tabungan Impian, Harga Jujur, OTO Poin, "Butuh keringanan".

## Frameworks & analyses (much wider than 2025)
- Root-cause tree plus R1–R7 causal chains, SCQA
- Survey with **6 hypotheses** (one-tailed z-tests, α=0.05; e.g. H1 z=−5.52, H5 z=2.43). The instrument sections A–G are each mapped to a hypothesis.
- STP with a PDB-style (Positioning/Differentiation/Branding) box
- TAM/SAM/SOM with formulas, cross-checked against SOF's own new financing
- Competitor analysis (main body plus a business-line heat-map) and a 13-app savings feature/rate benchmark
- SWOT/TOWS, 4P with implications, 5C with one insight per C, weighted PESTLE, Porter's 5 Forces with a response to each force
- Solution matrix with a "Passes on" row (how each step hands off to the next)
- Credit policy design: a Kol 1–5 grid, a profile A/B/C/Belum table with instalment caps and an explicit **plafon formula**
- Dream-asset simulation (petrol vs EV scooter vs EV car, with cost per km)
- Phased Gantt plus pilot gates and a **stop rule** (scale only while 30-day delinquency ≤ SOF's book)
- FMEA register of 26 risks (P×I, with owners)
- Assumption register, income statement, cash flow, NPV/IRR/ROI and payback, plus an explicit argument about which payback is right (1.1 years vs 4.4 years when loans are counted as investment).

## Numbers
- **Research:** online survey of **n=82** Gen Z aged 18–29, ~10 minutes, Likert plus numeric items. Profile: 93.9% students, 89% Jabodetabek. Key stats:
  - 74.4% want a vehicle within 2 years, but 54.9% have Rp0 saved
  - 52.4% have no savings target, while 91.5% say a goal helps
  - Only 32.9% understand SLIK
  - 63.4% are interested in save-first; 70.7% prefer it over paylater
  - SOF awareness is 32.9% (vs MUF 56.1% and Adira 52.4%); 80.5% trust bank-backed lenders
- **Market sizing:** TAM Rp59.6T/yr (6.41m motorcycles × 60% financed × Rp15.5m) → SAM Rp23.8T (×40% Gen Z share) → SOM Rp775B by Y3 (50k contracts; 3.2% of SAM, 6.6% of SOF's 2025 new financing). The contract ramp is 5k/20k/50k/65k/80k.
- **KPIs per pillar** (4 chips each):
  - T: 300k check-ups in Y1, 30% of "not ready" users reach Siap within 6 months, ≥60% Kelas OTO completion, −30% rejection complaints
  - A: 100k active plans, ≥6-month median streak, 25% reach their DP on time, ≥60% go on to apply
  - P: 50k contracts by Y3, ≤3 days to disbursement, ≥60% approved without a field survey, ≥20% take Dana Tunai or an upgrade
- **Cost per pillar** (in the solution matrix): T Rp0.8B build + Rp2.0B/yr; A no build + Rp0.9B/yr in rewards; P Rp0.6B integration + Rp2.0B/yr (≈Rp200k CAC).
- **Assumptions:** tenor mix 12/18/24/36 months at 11–14% flat; cost of funds 7.0%; credit loss 3.0% (stop rule at 3.5%); CAC Rp200k; e-KYC Rp25k; team of 6–11 FTE.
- **Financials:**
  - Cash from operations Rp3.5B (2027) → Rp175B (2031)
  - NPV Rp170.96B at 12%, IRR 82.3%, "ROI" 472.4%, operating payback 1.1 years, equity payback 4.4 years
  - **Caveat:** the terminal value (Rp279.6B, PV ≈Rp158.6B) is about 93% of the NPV. My check: the discounted flows sum to 170.97 ✓.
- **Sources:** about 60 references (OJK, LPS/BPS SNLIK, Bisnis, Kontan, AISI, Populix, SOF annual reports, plus Ashraf-Karlan-Yin 2006 as academic proof that commitment saving works). There are also footnoted model versions ("OTO TAP Financial Projection v8").

## Why it likely won
1. **It answers the case's literal question** (innovative product + regulation + sustainable growth + risk) point by point, and the design is **regulation-native**: no deposits at SOF, the 30% instalment cap per PADK 2/2026, POJK 22/2023 collections and UU PDP consent. Judges from a regulated lender will value this most.
2. **Sponsor-ecosystem leverage:** it uses the group's own assets (Jenius' 6.3m users, the SMBC funding line, 409 branches) with a "Group synergy" strip on every pillar page.
3. **Proof density:** a hypothesis-tested survey, a 60-source bibliography, an assumption register, FMEA, pilot gates and a stop rule, and a plafon formula worked through for a persona.
4. **Product realism:** hi-fi app screens make the idea feel shippable. Transparent decline reasons are a memorable, humane feature.
5. **It copies the previous winner's proven template** (see the comparison below) and then out-builds it on rigour.

## Weaknesses
- **The NPV depends on the terminal value** (~93% of the NPV). The "ROI 472.4%" and 1.1-year payback flatter the case by excluding the loan book, although the team discloses and argues this, which is good practice.
- **The sample is mismatched to the target:** 93.9% students and 58.5% earning under Rp3m, while the targeted segments are earners at ≥Rp3m.
- The **brand/communication plan is thin** for a "BRAND" competition. Awareness (32.9%) is a stated problem, but there is no media plan, no awareness KPI target and no campaign creative beyond the "campus & staff ambassadors" and "creators" lines in the Gantt.
- There is no sensitivity or scenario table (only a stop rule).
- It is very dense; skimming judges may struggle with the 4-row matrices.
- It is 52 pages long. The appendices balloon to around 24 pages, which is fine for this competition but would be risky where page limits are strict.

## Differences vs Wijaya Team house style
**Already very close to Wijaya's style:** an acronym solution with each letter mapped to a root cause, a KPI and a cost; a persona hook (Rani); the issue chain; a thin main body plus a heavy appendix; a sponsor-asset tie-in (Jenius, like QRIS/Blibli); a pilot plus stop rule (≈ Wijaya's stage gates); formula-shown sizing.

- **Hypothesis testing with z/p values**, with every survey section mapped to a hypothesis.
- An **FMEA register** (26 risks, P×I, owners).
- A **"Passes on" row** showing how each pillar hands its output to the next (a sequential funnel rather than parallel pillars).
- **Indonesian colloquial pillar names**.
- **Hi-fi app UI** inside a business case.
- An explicit **regulation mapping** (POJK, PADK, UU PDP).
- Porter's 5 Forces with a response to each force; a "Group synergy" strip.

**Wijaya does, they don't:** downside/base/upside cases and a sensitivity grid; a capital-at-risk ask; a "what we will NOT do" guardrails slide; a one-page infographic exec summary (theirs is prose); "Takeaway:" lines (they come close: each appendix figure ends with a one-line insight); a fixed bottom section tracker.

**Takeaways for IBCC BRAND UI**
- **The 2026 winner visibly reverse-engineered the 2025 winner.** It uses the same chapter list and sub-headings, the same figure set (root-cause tree, survey infographic, STP, sizing, competitor, SWOT/TOWS, 4P, solution matrix, giant-initial pillar pages with a 4-chip Impact bar, PRIORITY timeline, numbered risk plus matrix) and even the same sentence templates ("Through 4P analysis, X's current position is identical…", "By merging credibility with…", "transform from a '…' into a '…'"). **Lesson: start every IBCC BRAND UI entry from the previous winner's skeleton**, which is probably the guidebook's mandated format, and win on depth.
- **The bar is rising.** 2025 won with insight, creative coherence and a passable cash flow. 2026 needed hypothesis tests, FMEA, an assumption register, regulatory mapping and a ~60-source bibliography.
- **"BRAND" in the name does not mean a marketing case.** Prepare for any corporate case. Still, always end with a positioning shift ("from X to Y") and a branded, acronym-plus-tagline solution, because both winners did.
- Neither winner has a sensitivity/scenario grid or a one-page visual exec summary. Wijaya's strengths would fill those gaps.

## Reusable techniques
- Make **pillars a sequential journey** (check → save → own) with a "Passes on" row, so the acronym reads as a funnel instead of three disconnected programmes.
- Give the **survey a hypothesis structure**: one hypothesis per root cause, one-tailed z-test against 50% or a benchmark, and an instrument table mapping section → hypothesis → key result.
- Put a **"Group synergy" strip** on every pillar page showing the role of each sponsor-group entity.
- Run the **persona through a worked formula** (Rani's plafon calculation) so the policy becomes concrete.
- Add a **"Transparent decline" / humane-feature UI** moment. It is memorable for judges and signals consumer protection.
- Write a **regulation-native design**: name the regulation behind each constraint (POJK, PADK, UU PDP) and show the solution complying with it.
- Run an **FMEA of 20+ risks in the appendix**, put the top 5 in the body, and use a pilot cap, loss ceiling and written stop rule.
- **Pre-empt the financial objection:** show both paybacks (1.1 years operating vs 4.4 years equity) and argue which one is right.
- Make the **4P table** end in an "IMPLICATION" row that becomes a design requirement for the solution.
- Write **5C / Porter's** so each block ends in a one-line insight, and add a "response to each force" figure.
- Attach the **Turnitin report** when originality is checked.
- On the **cover, show the product in use**: a UI mockup with persona data, a photoreal hero image and the tagline.
