## Storyline pattern
- **Hook:** macro and policy rather than a human story: "only 35% of health facilities have adequate waste management" plus coal phase-out by 2040/2045 plus a WtE market that is still growing. It opens with an industry trend, not a persona.
- **Chain:** industry gap → the company's ambition (TBS2030 "green pivot", ARAH acquisition) → pain points (high cost/ton, fragmented compliance, energy and waste in silos) → **SCQ-like question box** ("How can TBS leverage existing capabilities while building a pipeline of innovation and revenue?") → issue tree → **YES!**.
- **Solution naming:** the acronym **YES!**, with an exclamation mark, exactly like Wijaya's FLOW!/TRUST!. Each letter is one business line: Y = asset play (coal → WtE), E = digital SaaS-like service (CaaS), S = pricing/vertical play. The title frames the case KPI ("Optimizing ARAH **EBITDA**"), so the answer is anchored to the sponsor's metric from the cover onward.
- **Impact:** each pillar slide ends in an "Impact" band with 4 big % or number tiles (e.g., 85% waste volume reduction, ~$3–5M revenue/yr, +35% reporting speed, −40% legal-violation risk, +36% EBITDA).
- **Close:** the timeline and financial slide is the last main slide. There is no separate conclusion or ask slide; the "Interpretation" box on slide 10 acts as the close.

## Frameworks & analyses
Issue tree (Objective/Issue/Sub-issue/Implication), TAM-SAM-SOM, competitor matrix, Porter's Five Forces (radar), PESTLE, SWOT→TOWS, **weighted scoring table** (competitive advantage, cost efficiency, EBITDA growth potential, execution feasibility), service journey map (8 steps), risk heat matrix + mitigation table, Gantt, revenue simulation, unit-margin simulation, cash flow + income statement, CAGR/IRR/ROI/PBP.

## Why it likely won
- **Answers the sponsor KPI directly** (EBITDA) and maps 3 pillars to 3 revenue mechanisms; judges from TBS can see their own assets reused (coal plants → WtE, ARAH acquisition, TBS2030).
- **Feasibility signals** are strong: partner logos, regulation citations, a Gantt per pillar, a risk matrix with per-pillar mitigation, and a **scoring table that justifies prioritisation**.
- **Quantified everywhere:** every pillar ends in an impact-number row, and there are unit economics per ton and a revenue simulation per tonnage.
- **Consulting polish:** consistent chrome, action titles, a tracker bar and source lines, which reads like a real advisory deck.

## Weaknesses
- **Financials don't reconcile.** In the income statement, Income Before Tax 37,201 − Tax 8,184 is shown as Net Income **−99,639** (should be +29,017). EBITDA is −55,113 while income before tax is positive, and there is no opex line. The main-slide revenue chart is **10× smaller** than the appendix (12,257 vs 122,571) and labelled "in million rupiah". "Total revenue 1.074 Billion" is really ~Rp1.07 trillion. Investment is 667B vs 677.68B.
- **PBP 2Y is not supported:** cumulative net income only turns positive in year 4 (2028) even before recovering the ~678B investment.
- SOM $5.2B (a third of SAM) is not derived and is implausibly large for ARAH.
- There is no explicit ask/budget-approval slide and no stage gates. The appendix "click this page" points to a spreadsheet that isn't linked in the PDF.
- The flattened-image PDF loses searchability, and small text is hard to read.
- The competitor matrix is duplicated (slide 4 and slide 19).

## Differences vs Wijaya Team house style
**They do, Wijaya usually doesn't:**
- **Weighted scoring table** of 5 candidate initiatives (4 criteria, /20) to justify why these pillars and not others. Wijaya jumps from root causes to pillars without showing rejected options.
- **Pillar = business line with its own revenue stream and its own impact band.** The solution is framed as a portfolio of 3 businesses (asset, SaaS, pricing) rather than 3 operational fixes.
- **Unit-margin-per-ton simulation** per pricing tier, in the appendix.
- **Service journey map** (8-step blueprint) for the digital pillar.
- Partner/stakeholder **logo rows** inside each pillar slide (real, current partners of the sponsor).
- English-only, image-only export.

**Wijaya does, they don't:**
- A human hook/persona (MetaMind opens on macro data).
- Formula + source + "assumption" tags on each number, scenario/sensitivity analysis and an assumption register. MetaMind shows none, and its numbers break.
- Pilot + stage gates + capital-at-risk + an explicit ask.
- A guardrails / "what we won't do" slide.
- A numbered appendix cross-referenced from the main slides (MetaMind's appendix is not cited from the main body).

**Shared:** acronym with "!" (YES! ≈ FLOW!/TRUST!), issue tree, sponsor-asset tie-in, action titles, bottom tracker, sponsor palette.

## Reusable techniques
- Put the **case KPI in the solution title** ("Optimizing ARAH EBITDA through YES!") and spell out the acronym on the cover.
- Close each pillar slide with a fixed **"Impact" band of 4 big-number tiles** (2 operational, 2 financial).
- Add a **"Key Driver" column** on each pillar slide: 2–3 cited market facts that justify the pillar, placed left of the solution.
- Use an **initiative scoring table** (competitive advantage / cost efficiency / KPI growth potential / feasibility, 1–5, total /20, remarks column) in the appendix to defend prioritisation in Q&A.
- **Gantt color-coded by pillar** (one shade per letter) on the same slide as the 4 headline return metrics (CAGR/IRR/ROI/PBP) plus an "Interpretation" box.
- Show a **revenue simulation table** (volume tiers → output → revenue) so judges can see the scaling logic.
- Use a **bottom 4-tab tracker** + top-left kicker + top-right sponsor logo as fixed chrome.
- **Anti-pattern to avoid:** a main-slide chart that disagrees with the appendix table. Generate both from one model, and recompute net income/EBITDA lines in code.
